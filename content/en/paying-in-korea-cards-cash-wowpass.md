Korea is one of the most card-friendly countries in the world. Locals pay for almost everything by card or phone, from a coffee to a taxi ride, and most shops that take cards accept foreign Visa and Mastercard. You still need a little cash, though, mainly for street food, traditional markets and topping up a transit card.

> **Quick answer:** Bring a **Visa or Mastercard** with low foreign transaction fees and know its **PIN**; it will cover most of your spending. Carry about **₩50,000–100,000 in cash** for markets, street stalls and transit card top-ups. A prepaid **WOWPASS** card is worth it if your home card charges high foreign fees or you would rather exchange cash on arrival, but it costs about ₩6,000 to issue and its transit balance is kept separate.

Fees and rules below are as of October 2026 and change from time to time, so check the current figure before you pay.

## Foreign credit and debit cards

**Where they work:** Visa and Mastercard are accepted at department stores, duty-free shops, hotels, chain restaurants and cafés, convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, Emart24), Olive Young and other cosmetics chains, and most attractions. As a rule of thumb, if a merchant accepts cards at all, a foreign Visa or Mastercard will usually go through. American Express, JCB and UnionPay are accepted in fewer places, so bring a Visa or Mastercard as your main card.

**Where they often fail:** Traditional markets (such as Gwangjang and Namdaemun), street food carts, some tent bars (pojangmacha) and small family-run restaurants may be cash-only or may have a card reader that only takes Korean cards.

**Tips for card payments:**

- **Know your PIN.** Korean terminals read the chip, and some ask for a PIN on foreign cards. Most of the time you will just sign on the screen or nothing at all, but a forgotten PIN can leave you stuck.
- **Pay in won.** If a terminal or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency (dynamic currency conversion), decline and choose **KRW**. The home-currency option usually comes with a worse exchange rate.
- **Bring two cards** from different networks or banks, in case one is blocked by your bank's fraud checks.
- **Tell your bank** you are traveling if it requires that.

**Apple Pay and Google Pay:** Apple Pay has worked in Korea since 2023, but acceptance is patchier than in Europe, the US or Japan because many Korean terminals do not read international contactless payments. Treat phone payments as a bonus and carry the physical card.

## Cash: how much to bring and where to get it

Most visitors find that **₩50,000–100,000** in cash covers a few days of street food, market shopping and transit top-ups in a big city. Carry more if you plan to eat mainly at markets or travel in rural areas.

Korean banknotes come in **₩1,000, ₩5,000, ₩10,000 and ₩50,000**, and coins in ₩10, ₩50, ₩100 and ₩500. Small notes are handy because street vendors and T-money machines can be slow to break a ₩50,000 note.

### Exchanging money

- **At the airport:** Bank exchange counters at Incheon and Gimpo are convenient, but their rates are usually not the best. Exchange only what you need for the first day or two.
- **In the city:** Licensed private money changers in tourist districts such as Myeongdong generally offer better rates than the airport. Compare the rate board and ask what you will receive before handing over cash.
- **At your home bank:** Worth comparing, but many banks outside Asia keep only small stocks of won and give poor rates.

### Using ATMs

Not every Korean ATM accepts foreign cards. Look for machines labeled **"Global ATM"** or with your card network's logo. They are common at airports, in major bank branches, and inside many convenience stores.

- **Fees:** Expect a fee charged by the Korean ATM operator plus whatever your own bank charges. Fees vary by bank and machine (as of 2026), so check the amount shown on screen before confirming.
- **Limits:** Single withdrawals are often capped somewhere between ₩400,000 and ₩1,000,000, depending on the machine.
- **Maintenance pauses:** Some ATMs stop working for a short window around midnight.
- **Choose KRW** if the machine offers to convert the amount into your home currency.

## WOWPASS: the prepaid card for tourists

WOWPASS is a prepaid card for foreign visitors that combines a payment card with a T-money transit card. You get it from WOWPASS kiosks at Incheon Airport, in hotels and in tourist districts, load it with foreign cash or by card, and pay with it like a normal card anywhere that takes cards in person.

| Item | WOWPASS (as of October 2026) |
|---|---|
| Card issuance | About ₩6,000 (raised from ₩5,000 in March 2026); slightly less if you reserve in the app |
| Top-up with cash at a kiosk | No fee; accepts KRW and around 15 foreign currencies, with the exchange margin built into the rate |
| Top-up by credit card in the app | Up to ₩200,000 per transaction; third-party guides report a fee of around 4%, so check the app before confirming |
| Maximum balance | ₩1,000,000 |
| Cash withdrawal at a WOWPASS kiosk | ₩1,000 per withdrawal, up to ₩100,000 at a time, in notes only |
| Card reissue | ₩3,000 |
| Balance validity | Several years from the last top-up |

**How the T-money part works:** The card holds two separate balances. Your payment balance pays in shops, and a separate T-money balance pays for subways, buses and taxis. You can top up the T-money side with cash at subway station machines and convenience stores like any T-money card. Moving money from the payment balance to the T-money balance through the app is possible but carries a small fee (₩500 since December 2025), and money cannot move back the other way.

**Is it worth it?** WOWPASS makes sense if:

- your home card charges a foreign transaction fee of around 3% or more,
- you would rather exchange cash on arrival and still pay by card, or
- you want one card for shopping and transit.

If you already have a fee-free travel card from your bank or a multi-currency card such as Wise or Revolut, you can skip WOWPASS and just buy a regular T-money card for transit.

## Paying for transit

Transit is where foreign cards are least useful.

- **Subway and buses:** You cannot tap a foreign credit card at Seoul's subway gates or on city buses. Use a **T-money card** (about ₩3,000–4,000 at convenience stores, topped up with cash) or the built-in T-money on a WOWPASS. See our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/) for fares and the unlimited **Climate Card** pass.
- **Ticket machines:** Since March 17, 2026, the ticket machines on Seoul Subway Lines 1–8 accept foreign Visa and Mastercard cards for single-journey tickets and for buying and loading the 1–7 day Climate Card. A surcharge of about 3.7% applies. This helps if you arrive with no cash, but it does not let you top up a regular T-money card by card.
- **Taxis:** Card readers are standard in Korean taxis, and foreign cards usually work. Taxis also take T-money. Keep some cash as a backup in rural areas.
- **Airport trains and KTX:** Buy AREX and KTX tickets at station counters or machines, or on the operators' websites; for getting into the city from Incheon, see our [airport transfer guide](../incheon-airport-to-seoul/).

## Tax refunds on shopping

Foreign visitors staying less than six months can claim back part of the 10% VAT on goods.

- **Minimum purchase:** ₩15,000 in one transaction at a store displaying a "Tax Free" sign.
- **Instant refund at the store:** Many shops take the tax off at the till if you show your **physical passport**. Instant refunds are capped at ₩1,000,000 per purchase and ₩5,000,000 per trip.
- **Otherwise:** Keep the refund slip and claim it at a refund kiosk in shopping districts or at the airport before you check in your bags. You must leave Korea within three months of the purchase.
- **What you get back:** After the refund operator's fee, usually about 4–7% of the price.

Rules can change, so check the signs and slips at the store.

## Tipping and other etiquette

- **No tipping.** Tipping is not expected in restaurants, cafés, taxis or hotels, and staff may try to give the money back. Some upscale hotels and restaurants add a service charge to the bill.
- **Pay at the counter.** In many Korean restaurants you take the bill to the cashier by the door when you leave instead of paying at the table.
- **Splitting the bill:** Many places will run several cards for one table if you ask, but busy small restaurants may prefer a single payment.

## FAQ

**Can I travel in Korea without any cash?**
Almost. In Seoul and Busan you can pay for nearly everything by card, and the subway ticket machines now take foreign cards. Cash is still the easiest way to top up T-money and pay at markets and street stalls, so most visitors carry a small amount.

**Do Korean shops accept American Express?**
Large hotels, department stores and duty-free shops usually do, but acceptance is lower than for Visa and Mastercard. Bring a Visa or Mastercard as well.

**Can I use Kakao Pay or Naver Pay as a tourist?**
These apps normally need a Korean phone number and a Korean bank account or card, so they are not practical for most short-term visitors. A foreign card or WOWPASS is simpler.

**Can I get my leftover WOWPASS money back?**
You can withdraw remaining balance in ₩1,000 notes at WOWPASS kiosks that dispense cash (₩1,000 fee per withdrawal), or keep it for your next trip. Amounts under ₩1,000 stay on the card.

**Is it better to exchange money before I go?**
Usually not. Rates at licensed money changers in Seoul are generally better than at home or at the airport. Bring enough for the first day, or plan to use a Global ATM on arrival.
