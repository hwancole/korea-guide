Korea has fast, reliable mobile data almost everywhere, including on the subway, but your home plan's roaming can be expensive. Visitors have three practical ways to get online: a **travel eSIM**, a **Korean prepaid SIM card** from one of the three carriers (SK Telecom, KT, LG U+), or a rented **pocket WiFi** router.

> **Quick answer:** If your phone supports eSIM, a **data-only travel eSIM** set up before you fly is the easiest and usually the cheapest option. Get a **carrier SIM or eSIM with a Korean number** if you need to receive texts in Korea (for example to sign up for some local apps). Rent **pocket WiFi** if you are a family or group sharing data across several phones and tablets, or your phone cannot take a second SIM.

Prices below are as of October 2026 and come from carrier and booking-platform listings. Promotions change often, so check the current price before paying.

## Option 1: Travel eSIM (easiest for most people)

An eSIM is a digital SIM profile you install by scanning a QR code. You can buy it online days before the trip, install it at home, and switch it on when you land, so you have data the moment the plane taxis in. Your normal SIM stays in the phone, which means you can still receive texts from your bank at home.

- **Price:** International eSIM sellers generally price Korea at about $10–25 for 5–7 days, with cheaper fixed-data plans (a few GB) and pricier unlimited ones (as of October 2026).
- **Network:** Most run on one of the three Korean carriers' networks, so coverage is the same nationwide.
- **Phone number:** Most travel eSIMs are **data only**. You can still use WhatsApp, LINE, KakaoTalk (if already registered) and internet calls, but you will not get a Korean number for incoming texts.

**Check before buying:** your phone must be **unlocked** and **eSIM-capable**. Most iPhones from the XS onward, recent Samsung Galaxy S and Z models, and Google Pixel 3 and later support eSIM, but carrier-locked phones and some regional models do not. On an iPhone, look for "Add eSIM" under Settings > Mobile/Cellular. Install the eSIM while you still have WiFi, because activation needs a connection.

## Option 2: Korean prepaid SIM from SKT, KT or LG U+

The three Korean carriers sell short-term tourist SIM cards at counters in the arrivals halls of Incheon, Gimpo and other airports, and through booking sites with airport pickup. Several also sell the same plans as eSIMs.

| Carrier | Unlimited-data tourist plans (as of October 2026) |
|---|---|
| SK Telecom | ₩6,500 (1 day), ₩18,000 (3 days), ₩27,500 (5 days), ₩35,000 (7 days), ₩38,500 (10 days), longer plans up to 30 days and beyond |
| LG U+ | ₩6,500 (1 day) up to ₩27,500 (5 days); optional prepaid voice credit |
| KT | Short and long plans (up to around 90 days); prices vary by product and seller |

Online sellers often discount these, and prices on booking platforms can be lower than at the counter.

**What you need at the counter:**

1. Your **original passport** (photos and photocopies may not be accepted).
2. An **unlocked phone**.
3. A **credit or debit card** to pay.

Staff usually insert the SIM, register it and test the connection for you.

**Korean phone number:** Carrier plans with voice give you a Korean **010** number once your passport is verified. Incoming calls and texts are generally free; outgoing calls and texts use a prepaid balance you top up. This number lets you receive verification texts for things like a new KakaoTalk account or some delivery and taxi apps. It generally will **not** pass Korean identity verification for banking or government services, which requires a Korean-registered identity.

**Downsides:** A physical SIM replaces your home SIM, so you will not get texts sent to your home number (keep the old SIM somewhere safe). If you land in the middle of the night, check that the counter at your terminal is 24 hours.

### Where are the carrier counters at Incheon Airport?

All three carriers have counters on the **arrivals level (1F)** of both terminals, and each has at least one desk open 24 hours: near **Arrival Hall F** in Terminal 1, and near **Exits 4 and 7** in Terminal 2, with extra desks open roughly 06:00–22:00 (as of mid-2026). Signs say "Roaming" or show the carrier logo. Exact desk positions move occasionally, so check the airport's "Telecommunications" listing or ask at the information desk.

## Option 3: Pocket WiFi (best for groups)

A pocket WiFi (often called a "WiFi egg") is a small battery-powered router running on a Korean carrier's LTE or 5G network. Your phones connect to it like home WiFi.

- **Price:** Around **₩3,000–4,000 per day** on booking platforms for unlimited or high-cap data, against undiscounted list prices of around ₩8,800 a day (as of October 2026).
- **Devices:** Usually up to 3–5 at once.
- **Battery:** Roughly 6–9 hours, so carry a power bank on long days.
- **Pickup and return:** At carrier or rental desks in the airport arrivals hall. Return it **before you go through departures security**, because there are no counters airside. Late returns are charged by the day.
- **Deposit:** Most rental companies ask for a credit card in your name to cover loss, damage or late return.

It is the cheapest per person when four people share one device, and it works with any phone or tablet. The catch is that everyone has to stay near the person carrying it, and nobody gets a Korean number.

## Which should you choose?

- **Solo or couple, phone supports eSIM:** a data-only travel eSIM.
- **Need a Korean number** (local app sign-ups, booking restaurants that text you, staying a month or more): a carrier SIM or carrier eSIM with voice.
- **Family or group of 3–5 traveling together:** one pocket WiFi, possibly plus an eSIM for the person who splits off.
- **Phone is locked or older:** pocket WiFi.
- **Only in Korea for a day or two on a layover:** free public WiFi plus a one-day carrier plan or a small eSIM.

## Free WiFi in Korea

Free WiFi is widespread: Incheon Airport, subway stations and many trains, cafés, convenience stores and public areas run by city governments (look for "Public WiFi Free" or "Seoul" networks). It is handy as a backup, but it can be slow at busy times and you lose it the moment you step away, so most visitors still want their own data for maps and translation apps.

## FAQ

**Do I need a Korean phone number as a tourist?**
Not usually. Maps (Naver Map, Kakao Map), translation and messaging all work on data. A Korean number helps mainly when an app or restaurant insists on texting a Korean number to verify you.

**Does Google Maps work in Korea?**
Only partly. Walking and driving directions are limited, so install Naver Map or Kakao Map. See our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/) for other apps that work well.

**Can I buy a SIM card at a convenience store?**
Some convenience stores sell prepaid data SIMs, but the airport counters or an online booking with airport pickup are simpler, because staff will register and test it for you.

**Can I use a Korean SIM in a phone bought in Korea or a locked phone?**
A carrier-locked phone will not accept another carrier's SIM or eSIM. Ask your home carrier to unlock it before you travel.

**Is roaming from my home carrier worth it?**
Compare first. Some home plans include Korea at a flat daily fee, which can beat buying anything locally for a very short trip. For trips of more than a few days, a local eSIM or SIM is almost always cheaper.
