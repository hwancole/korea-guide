If you rely on Google Maps at home, Korea is the one place where it will let you down. It finds places and plans subway and bus trips, but it will not walk or drive you anywhere turn by turn. Locals use two Korean apps instead, Naver Map and Kakao Map, and both have English modes that work well for visitors.

> **Quick answer:** As of October 2026, **Google Maps works only partly in South Korea**: place search, saved lists and public transit routes work, but there are **no turn-by-turn walking or driving directions**. Install **Naver Map** as your main navigation app (English, Japanese and Chinese menus, transit, walking and driving directions) and **Kakao Map** as a backup. Neither app needs an account or a Korean phone number for searching and directions.

Everything below is as of October 2026. App menus change with updates, and Google's Korean service may change during 2026 or later, so check the app store notes if something looks different.

## Why Google Maps is limited in Korea

Korean law restricts sending detailed government map data to servers outside the country, mainly for security reasons. Google processes routes on its overseas data centers, so for years it could show Korean maps (licensed from a local map provider) but could not calculate full walking and driving routes on them.

On **February 27, 2026**, the Korean government **conditionally approved** Google's request to export 1:5,000-scale base map data. The conditions include blurring sensitive military and security sites in imagery, removing or limiting coordinate data, and processing the data on servers run by Google's Korean partners before limited data leaves the country.

Approval is not the same as a launch. As of late October 2026, Korean media report that **Google has not announced when** full walking and driving navigation will arrive in Korea. Until it does, plan your trip around a Korean map app.

## Naver Map: the best main app for visitors

Naver Map is one of Korea's two big map apps and the one most travel guides recommend for foreigners. Its App Store listing lists **English, Japanese, Korean and Simplified Chinese**, and it offers transit, walking and driving directions plus English voice guidance for driving.

### How to switch Naver Map to English

1. Install **NAVER Map** from the App Store or Google Play (the iPhone version needs iOS 16 or later as of October 2026).
2. The app normally follows your phone's language. If it opens in Korean, tap your **profile icon** in the top corner, open **Settings** (the gear icon) and choose **Language**, then **English**.
3. Restart the app if some labels have not changed.

Some content stays in Korean even in English mode, such as certain shop names and notices. Since October 2024, Naver has used its Papago translation to show customer reviews and search filters in English, Japanese and Chinese.

### Getting directions

1. Tap the **route (directions)** button.
2. Enter a start point (or use your current location) and a destination.
3. Choose **public transport**, **walking** or **car** at the top.
4. For transit, the app shows travel time, transfers and walking distance, plus real-time subway and bus arrivals. It can also alert you when your stop is coming up.

**Tip for the subway:** Exit numbers, platform numbers and car numbers are shown as digits, so you can follow them without reading Korean. Our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/) explains fares, T-money and the Climate Card pass.

## Kakao Map: the backup worth having

Kakao Map is the other big Korean map app, made by the company behind KakaoTalk. Its App Store listing supports **English and Korean**, and it has transit, walking, cycling and driving directions with built-in turn-by-turn navigation.

- **Switching to English:** Open the menu (☰), go to **Settings** and look for **Language**. If the option does not appear, set your phone to English and restart the app. The Kakao Map website is mostly in Korean, so use the mobile app.
- **Real-time buses:** Kakao Map shows where your bus is on the map and when it will arrive, which many users find clearer than other apps.
- **Search by phone number:** Typing the last digits of a shop's phone number is often the quickest way to find a small restaurant whose English name does not match.
- **Limits:** Reviews and many business descriptions stay in Korean, and some transit notices appear in Korean even in English mode.

Having both apps installed helps when one cannot find a place or when an English name is spelled differently.

## How to search when you cannot type Korean

Searching is where most visitors get stuck, because Korean places often have several English spellings. These tricks work in both apps:

- **Copy and paste the Korean name.** Hotel booking sites, restaurant listings and even Google Maps usually show the Korean name. Paste it straight into Naver or Kakao Map.
- **Use the road-name address.** Since 2014, Korea's official addresses use road names and building numbers (for example, a street name ending in "-ro" or "-gil"). Pasting the full Korean address is the most reliable search.
- **Try the romanized name first,** then a shorter version. "Gyeongbokgung" works better than "Gyeongbok Palace Seoul Korea".
- **Search for the nearest subway station** if a small shop does not show up, then walk from there with the map.
- **Open shared links.** If your hotel or a friend sends a Naver Map or Kakao Map link, tapping it opens the exact spot.

## How to use Google Maps alongside them

Google Maps is still useful in Korea, just not for the last few hundred meters:

- **Before your trip,** save restaurants, cafés and sights in Google Maps lists, and note each place's Korean name and address.
- **For overview,** use it to see what is near your hotel or how far apart neighborhoods are.
- **For transit,** it can plan subway and bus routes, but double-check transfers and exits in Naver Map, which has more detailed and real-time data.
- **For the final walk,** switch to Naver or Kakao Map rather than following Google's dotted lines.

## Tips for a smoother trip

- **Get mobile data.** Map apps need a connection for routes and real-time arrivals. Compare options in our [SIM card, eSIM and pocket WiFi guide](../korea-sim-card-esim-pocket-wifi/).
- **Install the apps before you fly** and set the language while you have good Wi-Fi.
- **Allow location access** so the apps can show the right exit and starting point.
- **Show the Korean address to taxi drivers.** Drivers know Korean addresses and place names better than English ones.
- **Mind the compass.** In dense areas like Myeongdong or Gangnam, start walking for 20–30 meters and check that your dot moves the right way before following the route.

## FAQ

**Does Google Maps work in Korea in 2026?**
Partly. As of October 2026 it shows places, saved lists and public transit routes, but not turn-by-turn walking or driving directions. The government approved Google's map data export in February 2026, but Google has not announced a date for full navigation.

**Do I need a Korean phone number for Naver Map or Kakao Map?**
No. You can search and get directions without logging in. An account is needed only to save favorites, and Kakao allows signing up with an email and a foreign phone number.

**Which is better for foreigners, Naver Map or Kakao Map?**
Most visitors find Naver Map easier because it supports more languages and shows English transit details, such as station names and exits, more completely. Kakao Map is a good second app, especially for real-time bus tracking.

**Can I use Naver Map for driving in Korea?**
Yes. Naver Map offers driving directions with English voice guidance, which makes it the usual choice for visitors renting a car. Kakao Map also has built-in turn-by-turn navigation.

**Does Apple Maps work better than Google Maps in Korea?**
Do not count on it. Korean apps have the most complete local data, so use Naver Map or Kakao Map as your main navigation app whatever phone you have.

**Will Google Maps get walking directions in Korea soon?**
Possibly, but there is no official date. Check recent news before your trip, and keep a Korean map app installed either way.
