Gwangjang Market (광장시장) is Seoul's best-known traditional market for street food. It opened in 1905 and is a short walk from Jongno 5-ga Station. Its covered food alleys are packed with stalls where you sit on a bench and eat mung bean pancakes, mini gimbap rolls, raw beef and noodle soup cooked right in front of you.

> **Quick answer:** Go to **Jongno 5-ga Station (Line 1), Exit 7 or 8**, and the market is a couple of minutes' walk. The food stalls open daily from morning until late evening, roughly **9 AM to 10–11 PM** as of November 2026, while the fabric and hanbok shops close around 6 PM and on Sundays. Most dishes cost **₩4,000–₩10,000**; yukhoe (beef tartare) at a sit-down restaurant costs more. Bring some cash in small notes, check the menu board price before you order, and order the item by its exact name.

## Where is Gwangjang Market and how do you get there?

The address is **88 Changgyeonggung-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul** (서울 종로구 창경궁로 88). It sits between Jongno street to the north and the Cheonggyecheon Stream to the south, near Dongdaemun.

| Station | Line | Exit | Walk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jongno 5-ga (종로5가) | Line 1 | Exit 7 or 8 | about 2 minutes |
| Euljiro 4-ga (을지로4가) | Lines 2 and 5 | Exit 4, then cross the Cheonggyecheon Stream | about 5–7 minutes |

Search "광장시장" or "Gwangjang Market" in Naver Map or Kakao Map rather than Google Maps for accurate walking directions (see our [guide to maps in Korea](../google-maps-korea-naver-kakao-map/)). Driving is not worth it: nearby car parks are small and busy. For the subway itself, see the [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/).

## Opening hours

Hours are set by each stall and shop, not by the market as a whole, so treat these as a guide (as of November 2026):

| Section | Typical hours | Closed |
|---|---|---|
| Food stalls and restaurants | about 8:30–9 AM until 10–11 PM | open daily; individual stalls take days off |
| Fabric, bedding and hanbok shops | about 8:30 AM–6 PM | most close on Sundays |
| Vintage clothing section (upstairs) | roughly 10 AM–7 PM | varies by shop |

**When to go:** Weekday mornings and early afternoons are the calmest. From around 6 PM, and all day on weekends, the central food alley fills up and you may wait for a bench. On Sundays the food side is open but the rest of the market is quiet, which can feel half-closed.

## What to eat at Gwangjang Market

The centre of the market is a crossroads of food stalls under a high roof. Each stall has a counter with benches around it; you sit where you order. These are the classic dishes:

| Dish | Korean | What it is | Typical price (Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bindaetteok | 빈대떡 | Thick mung bean pancake with pork and sprouts, fried crisp in oil. The market's signature. | ₩5,000–₩7,000 each |
| Mayak gimbap | 마약김밥 | Finger-sized rice rolls with carrot and pickled radish, served with a mustard and soy dip | ₩3,000–₩5,000 a plate |
| Yukhoe | 육회 | Seasoned raw beef with pear and egg yolk, served in sit-down restaurants in "yukhoe alley" | about ₩20,000–₩25,000; yukhoe bibimbap about ₩10,000–₩12,000 |
| Kalguksu | 칼국수 | Hand-cut noodle soup, often in a light anchovy or vegetable broth | about ₩6,000–₩8,000 |
| Sundae | 순대 | Korean blood sausage with glass noodles; sometimes served as a mixed plate with offal | ₩7,000–₩8,000 plain, more for a mixed plate |
| Tteokbokki | 떡볶이 | Rice cakes in sweet chilli sauce | ₩4,000–₩5,000 |
| Mandu | 만두 | Steamed or fried dumplings, usually ordered by the plate | ₩5,000–₩7,000 |
| Hotteok | 호떡 | Sweet filled pancake, a good dessert | ₩2,000–₩3,000 |
| Makgeolli | 막걸리 | Rice wine, the usual drink with bindaetteok | about ₩5,000 a bottle |

Prices are set by each stall and have risen over the last few years, so check the board. The ranges above come from recent menus and Korean news reports in 2025–2026.

**Bindaetteok:** Look for stalls with a stone grinder turning soaked mung beans at the front. The pancake is fried to order in a shallow pool of oil, so it is crunchy outside and soft inside. Dip it in the soy sauce and onion pickle on the table.

**Mayak gimbap:** The name means "narcotic gimbap", a joke about how addictive it is. It is simple, cheap and easy to eat while you walk around.

**Yukhoe:** Gwangjang is famous in Korea for raw beef. The yukhoe restaurants are in a small alley near the east side of the market, close to Jongno 5-ga Exit 7. They are proper restaurants with tables, menus and card machines, and are usually busier at dinner. If you are unsure about raw meat, the yukhoe bibimbap is a gentler start.

**Kalguksu:** One of the noodle stalls was featured in Netflix's *Street Food: Asia* (2019), and its counter often has a queue. Other noodle stalls nearby serve a very similar bowl with less waiting.

### A simple plan for two people

1. Share one **bindaetteok** and a bottle of makgeolli at a pancake stall.
2. Walk over and share a plate of **mayak gimbap**.
3. Sit at a noodle stall for one bowl of **kalguksu** or a plate of **mandu**.
4. Finish with a **hotteok**.

That comes to roughly ₩25,000–₩35,000 for two people as of November 2026, and leaves room for yukhoe if you want a bigger meal.

## Cash or card?

Bring cash, and some smaller notes (₩1,000 and ₩5,000). Here is how it works at Gwangjang as of November 2026:

- **Restaurants and indoor shops** usually take cards, including foreign cards.
- **Food stalls (the benches in the alleys)** are mixed. Seoul introduced a card-payment service for stalls that could not take cards in 2025, but some still ask for cash or a Korean bank transfer, which visitors cannot easily do.
- A prepaid travel card such as WOWPASS works wherever Korean cards do, but it does not help at a cash-only stall.

About ₩20,000–₩30,000 in cash per person covers a stall meal. See our [guide to paying in Korea](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/) for ATMs and travel cards.

## How to avoid being overcharged

Gwangjang has had repeated, well-publicised complaints about some food stalls since 2023: small portions for the price, a cheaper dish swapped for a pricier mixed plate, or a "minimum order" that is not on the menu. Seoul City and Jongno-gu have responded with several measures, as of November 2026:

- **Mystery shoppers**, including foreign ones, who check prices, portions, hygiene and service. Seoul ran rounds in 2024, in late 2025, and an intensive two-month inspection announced in May 2026.
- A **real-name system for stalls**, with penalty points and business suspensions for violations; stalls with repeated violations can lose their permit.
- **QR menus** with portion photos in around 20 languages, introduced in July 2025.
- Inspections of **price display** rules, with fines for serious violations.

Most visits go smoothly, but these habits help:

1. **Check the price on the board before you sit down.** Point to the item on the board or the QR menu.
2. **Order the item by its exact name and quantity**, for example "sundae il-inbun" (순대 1인분, one portion of sundae). If you are offered a "mixed" plate (모둠, modeum), it is a different, more expensive dish; say no if you do not want it.
3. **Ask the total before you pay**: "Eolmayeyo?" (얼마예요?, how much is it?).
4. **Prefer stalls that show prices clearly** and take cards.
5. If you are clearly overcharged, you can contact the **1330 Korea Travel Hotline** (call 1330, or +82-2-1330 from a foreign phone), which offers interpretation and handles tourist complaints.

## Beyond the food

- **Vintage clothing market:** Upstairs on the second floor is a large secondhand and vintage clothing section, popular with students and stylists. Prices are low and bargaining is limited.
- **Fabric, bedding and hanbok:** Gwangjang started as a textile market and still has hundreds of fabric and hanbok shops. Many make custom hanbok to order.
- **New shops:** In recent years the market has added a large Starbucks with a rooftop and, in April 2026, an Olive Young store on the second floor. See our [Olive Young shopping guide](../olive-young-shopping-guide/).
- **Nearby:** Cheonggyecheon Stream is a minute's walk south, and Dongdaemun Design Plaza is about 15 minutes east on foot. Ikseon-dong's hanok alleys are about 10 minutes west.

## FAQ

**Is Gwangjang Market open on Sundays?**
Yes, the food stalls and restaurants open on Sundays. Most fabric and hanbok shops close, so the non-food parts are quiet.

**What time do the food stalls close?**
Most stay open until about 10–11 PM. Some stalls close earlier on quiet nights, so arrive by 8 PM if you want the full choice.

**How much money should I bring?**
Plan on ₩15,000–₩25,000 per person for a stall meal with a drink, more for yukhoe. Carry at least ₩20,000 in cash in case a stall does not take foreign cards.

**Is the food safe?**
Food is cooked fresh in front of you, and the market is regularly inspected for hygiene. Raw beef (yukhoe) is a normal dish in Korea, but skip it if you are pregnant or have a weak immune system.

**Is it vegetarian-friendly?**
Partly. Bindaetteok usually contains pork, and broths often use anchovy. Mayak gimbap is often vegetarian, as are hotteok and some vegetable pancakes. Ask "gogi isseoyo?" (고기 있어요?, is there meat?).

**Is Gwangjang Market worth visiting?**
For a first trip to Seoul, yes. It is the easiest place to try many classic Korean street foods in one stop. Go hungry, go early, and order by the menu.
