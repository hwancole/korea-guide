Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌 한옥마을) is a hillside neighbourhood of traditional Korean houses between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces. It is not a museum or theme park: around 6,000 people live there, and the most photographed lanes are their front doors. Because of heavy crowds, Seoul's Jongno District now limits when tourists may walk through the busiest part.

> **Quick answer:** Bukchon is **free** to visit, but in the **Red Zone** around **Bukchon-ro 11-gil** (the famous sloping lane with the classic rooftop view), tourists may only visit from **10 AM to 5 PM**. Sightseeing there between 5 PM and 10 AM the next morning can lead to a **₩100,000 fine**, enforced since **1 March 2025** and still in force as of November 2026. The rest of Bukchon has no time limit, but it is still residential, so stay quiet, don't touch gates or sit on steps, and don't fly drones. Take **Line 3 to Anguk Station, Exit 2 or 3**.

## The visiting-hours rule at a glance

| Item | Rule (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|
| Where | Red Zone: the Bukchon-ro 11-gil area, about 34,000 m² of alleys near Jeongdok Public Library in Samcheong-dong and Gahoe-dong |
| Tourist hours | 10:00–17:00, every day including weekends and holidays |
| Restricted time | 17:00 to 10:00 the next morning |
| Penalty | Fine of ₩100,000 (about US$70) after a warning |
| Enforced since | 1 March 2025 (trial period from 1 November 2024) |
| Entry fee | None |
| Who checks | Bukchon guidance officers on patrol, who ask visitors to leave first |

The rule targets **tourism activity**: walking around to look, stopping to take photos or video, and lingering in the lanes. Officers give a warning first and only fine people who keep sightseeing after being asked to leave. Jongno District said in autumn 2025 that no fines had yet been issued in the first year, because most visitors left when asked, and that the current hours would stay in place for now. Treat the hours as a real rule, not a suggestion.

## The three zones

In July 2024, Seoul designated Bukchon as a special tourism management area and split the busiest lanes into three zones. Only the Red Zone has a time limit.

| Zone | Area | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Red Zone | Bukchon-ro 11-gil area | Tourists allowed 10:00–17:00 only; fine outside those hours |
| Orange Zone | Bukchon-ro 5-gil and the Gye-dong-gil area | No time limit; staff may guide crowds |
| Yellow Zone | Bukchon-ro 12-gil | No time limit; monitored for noise and crowding |

In practice, the Red Zone is the part most people come for: the steep lane of tiled roofs with Namsan Seoul Tower in the distance, and the narrow alleys around it. Signs on the streets mark the zone and the hours. If you arrive after 5 PM, you can still walk Gye-dong-gil, Samcheong-dong's cafés and the streets near the palaces, but skip the Red Zone lanes.

## Who is exempt?

The rule is aimed at tour groups and sightseers, not at people who live or do business there. According to Jongno District, these people may be in the Red Zone outside tourist hours:

- Residents of the Red Zone, plus their families and friends visiting them
- Merchants, and customers going to a shop, restaurant or café in the zone
- Overnight guests at a hanok stay or guesthouse in the zone
- People simply passing through without sightseeing
- Vehicles not used for tourism

The exemption does **not** cover sightseeing. If you are staying at a hanok guesthouse in Bukchon, you can walk back to it at 10 PM, but stopping in the lanes to take photos counts as tourism and can still be fined. If an officer stops you, explain that you are going to your accommodation or a specific shop and keep walking.

## Tour and charter buses

Large buses used to park along Bukchon-ro and block the narrow roads. Jongno District has restricted charter (tour) buses on about 2.3 km of Bukchon-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Bukchon-ro 4-gil and Changdeokgung 1-gil, with full enforcement announced from January 2026. Commuter, school and local village buses are exempt.

Since late November 2025, tour buses may only drop off and pick up at three designated points, and visitors walk in from there:

| Drop-off point | Location |
|---|---|
| National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MMCA Seoul) | in front of the museum, Sogyeok-dong |
| Opposite Changdeokgung Palace | Waryong-dong, across the road from the palace |
| Tapgol Park, west gate | Jongno 2-ga |

If you book a group tour, expect a 5–15 minute walk from the bus to the hanok lanes. Ordinary city buses, taxis and the subway are not affected.

## A simple walking route

This route covers the classic views in about 1.5–2 hours at an easy pace, ending before the 5 PM cutoff if you start by 2:30 PM:

1. **Anguk Station Exit 3** and walk up Bukchon-ro to the **Bukchon Cultural Center** on Gyedong-gil for a map.
2. Walk north on **Gye-dong-gil**, a lively old street with small shops and cafés (Orange Zone, no time limit).
3. Cross west to **Bukchon-ro 11-gil** (Red Zone) for the sloping lane of hanok roofs. This is the busiest spot; go between 10 AM and noon on a weekday for fewer people.
4. Continue to **Gahoe-dong 31** (가회동 31번지), a cluster of hanok with views over the rooftops.
5. Head down toward **Samcheong-dong** for cafés and galleries, then walk to **Gyeongbokgung** (see our [palace guide](../gyeongbokgung-palace-guide/)).

**Best time:** Weekday mornings shortly after 10 AM. Weekend afternoons are crowded, and many visitors in hanbok head here after the palaces. In winter the lanes are icy and steep after snow, so wear shoes with grip (see [what to wear in Korean winter](../korea-winter-weather-what-to-wear/)).

## Etiquette: what not to do in Bukchon

Residents and the district have put up signs asking for quiet. These points apply in every zone, not just the Red Zone:

- **Keep your voice down.** Talk softly and don't play music. Signs say "Please be quiet" and "Please talk softly".
- **Don't open gates, peek inside or climb steps** to houses. Signs saying "Do not enter" or "Do not come up" mark private property.
- **Don't sit on doorsteps or lean on walls and doors** for photos.
- **No smoking and no litter.** There are few bins, so carry your rubbish out.
- **No drones.** Central Seoul around Cheong Wa Dae and the palaces is a restricted no-fly area, so flying here needs special permission.
- **Photos are fine from the street**, but don't photograph residents or point cameras into open doors and windows. Large tripods and photo shoots block the lanes.
- **Wearing hanbok is welcome.** Hanbok rental shops near Anguk and Gyeongbokgung are popular, and wearing one also gets you free entry to the palaces.

If you can't make the 10 AM–5 PM window, these places have hanok without the same restrictions:

## FAQ

**Is Bukchon Hanok Village free?**
Yes. There is no entrance fee or ticket. It is a public residential neighbourhood.

**Can I visit Bukchon at night?**
You can walk most of Bukchon at night, but not the Red Zone around Bukchon-ro 11-gil, where sightseeing is only allowed from 10 AM to 5 PM. Outside those hours, you risk a warning and a ₩100,000 fine.

**Is Bukchon open on Mondays?**
Yes, the village is open every day. Only the Bukchon Cultural Center closes on Mondays, and some private museums and shops have their own days off.

**Do foreign tourists really get fined?**
The fine applies to Korean and foreign tourists alike. Officers warn first and fine only people who keep sightseeing after being told to leave, so the simple way to avoid it is to visit between 10 AM and 5 PM.

**I'm staying at a hanok guesthouse in Bukchon. Can I go back after 5 PM?**
Yes. Overnight guests are exempt, but only for getting to and from your accommodation. Stopping to take photos in the Red Zone after hours still counts as sightseeing.

**How long do I need?**
About 1–2 hours for the main lanes, or half a day if you combine it with Gyeongbokgung or Changdeokgung.

**Which subway station is closest?**
Anguk Station on Line 3. Use Exit 2 or 3.
