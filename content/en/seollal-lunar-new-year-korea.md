Seollal is Korean Lunar New Year, the biggest family holiday of the year alongside Chuseok. Millions of people travel to their hometowns, many small businesses close for a few days, and Seoul becomes noticeably quieter. It is still a perfectly good time to visit, as long as you plan around a handful of closures and book trains early.

> **Quick answer:** In 2027, Seollal falls on **Sunday, February 7**. The public holiday runs from **Saturday, February 6 to Monday, February 8**, plus a **substitute holiday on Tuesday, February 9**, so Koreans get four days off. Expect closed banks, many closed family-run restaurants and shops (especially on the 7th), department stores shut for one or two days, and sold-out KTX trains. Palaces, big tourist areas, convenience stores, chain cafés, the subway and most hotels keep running, and royal palaces have offered **free admission** over recent Seollal holidays.

Dates below come from the official 2027 calendar published in June 2026. Opening details for shops, museums and trains are based on how the February 2026 holiday worked and are a guide as of November 2026. Specific 2027 notices usually appear in January.

## Seollal 2027 dates at a glance

| Date | Day | Status |
|---|---|---|
| February 6, 2027 | Saturday | Holiday (Seollal eve). Peak outbound travel from Seoul |
| February 7, 2027 | Sunday | **Seollal day.** The most closures of the holiday |
| February 8, 2027 | Monday | Holiday (day after Seollal). Some shops reopen |
| February 9, 2027 | Tuesday | Substitute holiday. Return traffic peaks |
| February 10, 2027 | Wednesday | Normal working day. Some sites that opened over the holiday may close for a day |

Seollal follows the lunar calendar, so the date moves every year. It usually lands between late January and mid-February. The substitute holiday exists because one of the three holiday days falls on a Sunday.

## What closes and what stays open

The pattern is simple: big tourist infrastructure stays open, small family-run businesses close, and **Seollal day itself (February 7)** is the quietest day.

| Place | During Seollal (based on the 2026 holiday) |
|---|---|
| Royal palaces and Jongmyo Shrine | Open, and free over the 2026 holiday |
| Subway, city buses, airport trains | Running normally, often on a weekend or holiday timetable |
| KTX and intercity buses | Running, but sold out on peak days |
| Convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven) | Open, most 24 hours |
| Chain cafés and fast food in tourist areas | Mostly open, some with shorter hours |
| Department stores | Closed for **two days**, usually Seollal eve and Seollal day, or Seollal day and the day after |
| Outlets and malls | Most closed or opening late on Seollal day only |
| Large supermarkets (E-Mart, Lotte Mart, Homeplus) | About a third of branches closed on Seollal day |
| Banks and post offices | Closed for all holiday days. ATMs keep working |
| Traditional markets and small restaurants | Many close for two to four days |
| Hotels, guesthouses, tourist information centers | Open |

### Department stores

In February 2026, Lotte, Shinsegae and Hyundai closed most of their stores for two days: most branches shut on Seollal eve and Seollal day, while flagship stores such as Shinsegae's main store and Hyundai Apgujeong shut on Seollal day and the day after. Outlets generally closed only on Seollal day, and Lotte World Mall stayed open with later opening hours on Seollal day. Expect a similar split in 2027, and check the store's own notice in January if you are planning a shopping day. If shopping is a priority, schedule it for February 5 or February 10 or later.

### Restaurants and markets

Family-run restaurants in residential neighborhoods are the most likely to close, often for the whole holiday. Places that rely on tourists, such as **Myeongdong**, **Insadong**, **Hongdae** and the area around big hotels, are much more likely to stay open. Hotel restaurants, food courts in malls that are open, and chains are the safest options on February 7. Traditional markets such as Gwangjang Market thin out a lot, so save our [Gwangjang Market food guide](../gwangjang-market-food-guide/) for another day of your trip.

Before you go somewhere specific, check its listing on Naver Map or Kakao Map. Many owners post holiday closures there. See our [maps guide](../google-maps-korea-naver-kakao-map/) if you have not set them up.

### Money

Banks and currency exchange booths in banks close for the holiday. Exchange cash beforehand, or rely on cards and an ATM that accepts foreign cards. Most shops, taxis and restaurants that are open take foreign cards. Our [payments guide](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/) explains the options.

## Palaces, museums and free admission

Seollal is one of the best times to see Seoul's palaces. For the 2026 holiday (February 14 to 18), the Korea Heritage Service made admission free at:

- **Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung and Changgyeonggung** palaces
- **Jongmyo Shrine**, which normally requires a timed guided tour, was open for free viewing
- 22 Joseon royal tombs

The **Secret Garden (Huwon) at Changdeokgung** was not included and still needed a paid ticket. All of these sites then closed for a day straight after the holiday, so do not plan a palace visit for the first working day after Seollal.

Free entry over Seollal has been repeated in recent years, but the 2027 notice is normally published in late January. Confirm it on the Korea Heritage Service or palace websites before you go. Palaces get busy with families, and many visitors wear hanbok. Our [Gyeongbokgung guide](../gyeongbokgung-palace-guide/) covers the layout and the guard ceremony.

Museums such as the **National Museum of Korea** and the **National Folk Museum** run holiday programs with traditional games, crafts and New Year greetings. In 2026 these events were mostly free and needed no booking. Check whether a museum is open on Seollal day itself, as some close that one day.

## Trains, buses and flights over Seollal

Getting around Seoul is easy during Seollal. Getting **out of** Seoul is the hard part.

- **KTX and other trains** are sold in a special holiday booking round, not on the usual schedule. For Seollal 2026, Korail announced the booking on January 7, opened it from January 15 (priority groups) and January 19 to 21 (everyone), and required payment within a few days or the booking was cancelled. Booking was done on the Korail website or the Korail Talk app, and the official notice said you needed to be a registered railway member. Seats on the busiest days sold out quickly.
- **Peak days** are the day before the holiday and Seollal eve (leaving Seoul), and the last two holiday days (returning). In 2027 that means roughly **February 5 to 6** outbound and **February 8 to 9** back to Seoul.
- **Intercity and express buses** also sell out, and highways are heavily congested.
- **Domestic flights**, such as to Jeju, are expensive and sell out early.

If you want to travel between cities, the easiest plan is to travel against the flow: be in Seoul on Seollal eve when everyone leaves, and avoid heading back to Seoul on February 8 and 9. Or move a few days before or after the holiday. For booking basics, see our [Seoul to Busan KTX guide](../seoul-to-busan-ktx/) and [Seoul to Jeju guide](../seoul-to-jeju-island/).

Incheon Airport gets very busy as Koreans fly abroad over long holidays. Allow extra time on February 5 and 6 and on February 9 and 10. See our [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](../incheon-airport-to-seoul/) for the train and bus options.

## Seollal traditions worth knowing

You will see these around Seoul even if you are not visiting a Korean family:

- **Sebae**: children and younger family members bow deeply to elders and receive *sebaetdon* (New Year money) in an envelope.
- **Tteokguk**: soup with thin slices of rice cake, eaten on Seollal morning. Eating it is said to make you one year older. Many restaurants serve it all winter.
- **Charye**: a memorial table of food for ancestors, set up at home on Seollal morning.
- **Yunnori**: a board game played with four wooden sticks. Palaces, museums and folk villages often set up games for visitors.
- **Hanbok**: families and tourists wear traditional clothes, especially at palaces.

The greeting is **"Saehae bok mani badeuseyo"** (새해 복 많이 받으세요), which means "receive lots of luck in the new year". Shop staff and hotel workers will appreciate it.

## Is Seollal a good time to visit Korea?

It depends on your trip:

- **Good if** you want quieter streets in Seoul, free palace entry, traditional events, and you are staying mostly in one city.
- **Less good if** your plan relies on shopping at department stores, eating at small local restaurants, crossing the country by train on peak days, or doing banking.

Early February is also cold, often below freezing in Seoul. Our [winter weather guide](../korea-winter-weather-what-to-wear/) explains what to pack.

## Tips for a smooth Seollal trip

1. **Plan big shopping and market visits** for the days before February 6 or after February 9.
2. **Book trains as soon as the holiday booking opens** in January, or avoid traveling between cities on peak days.
3. **Exchange cash before the holiday** if you need it.
4. **Pick a hotel near a tourist area** such as Myeongdong, Jongno or Hongdae, where more places stay open.
5. **Keep convenience stores in mind** for simple meals and snacks on Seollal day.
6. **Check opening notices in January** for any specific palace, museum or store you care about.

## FAQ

**When is Seollal in 2027?**
Sunday, February 7, 2027. The public holiday is February 6 to 8, with a substitute holiday on Tuesday, February 9.

**Is everything closed in Seoul during Seollal?**
No. The subway, palaces, major tourist streets, convenience stores, chain cafés and hotels stay open. Banks, many small family restaurants, traditional markets and department stores close for some or all of the holiday. February 7 has the most closures.

**Are palaces free during Seollal?**
They were free over the 2026 holiday, as in previous years, except for the Secret Garden at Changdeokgung. The 2027 decision is usually announced in late January.

**Can foreigners book KTX tickets for Seollal?**
The holiday booking round is held online through the Korail website and Korail Talk app, and the 2026 notice said bookings were for registered railway members. Seats on peak days go fast. If you miss out, try again later for cancellations or travel on off-peak days.

**Does the Seoul subway run during Seollal?**
Yes. It runs every day of the holiday, usually on a holiday timetable.

**Is Seollal the same as Chinese New Year?**
It is the same lunar new year date, but Korean traditions, food and customs are different. In Korea, it is mainly a quiet family holiday rather than a street festival with fireworks.

**Who can I call for help in English?**
The Korea Travel Helpline, 1330 (+82-2-1330 from a foreign phone), answers in English and other languages, including over the holiday.
