Seoul is huge, but most of what first-time visitors want to see sits in a fairly small area north of the Han River: the palaces, Myeongdong, Insadong, Dongdaemun and N Seoul Tower. Where you sleep matters less for distance than for **which subway lines you are on** and **how easy it is to get there from the airport with luggage**.

> **Quick answer:** For a first trip, stay in **Myeongdong** (central, Line 4, airport buses stop there) or **Jongno/Insadong** (closest to the palaces, Lines 1, 3 and 5). Pick **Hongdae** if you want nightlife, younger crowds and a direct train from Incheon Airport. Stay near **Seoul Station** if you are taking the KTX to Busan or arriving on the AREX Express. Choose **Gangnam** only if your plans are mostly south of the river.

Station names and lines below are as of October 2026. Hotel prices change by season and day of week, so this guide compares areas rather than quoting room rates.

## Myeongdong: the easiest base for a first trip

Myeongdong is Seoul's best-known shopping district and the default choice for many first-time visitors. Its streets are packed with cosmetics shops, clothing chains and money changers, and in the evening, food stalls set up along the main lanes.

- **Getting around:** Myeongdong Station is on **Line 4**, which runs straight to Seoul Station (two stops) and Dongdaemun. Euljiro 1-ga Station on **Line 2**, the loop line, is a short walk away and reaches Hongdae, Gangnam and Seongsu without a transfer.
- **Nearby:** Namsan and N Seoul Tower, Namdaemun Market, the Lotte and Shinsegae department stores, and Deoksugung Palace and City Hall within walking distance or one stop.
- **Downsides:** Very touristy, crowded on weekends, and restaurants on the main lanes are aimed at visitors. It gets quiet late at night once shops close.

**Choose it if:** it is your first time in Seoul, you want to shop for K-beauty, or you have only a few days and want everything close.

## Jongno, Insadong and Ikseon-dong: closest to the palaces

The old center of Seoul lies north of Myeongdong. **Gyeongbokgung**, **Changdeokgung**, **Bukchon Hanok Village**, Insadong's craft shops and the small hanok alleys of Ikseon-dong are all here.

- **Getting around:** **Anguk Station (Line 3)** is the closest stop for Insadong, Bukchon and Changdeokgung. **Jongno 3-ga** connects Lines 1, 3 and 5, so you can reach most of the city with at most one transfer.
- **Where to sleep:** Mid-range hotels sit along Jongno and around Gwanghwamun, and the area has several **hanok guesthouses** (traditional Korean houses) if you want to sleep on a floor mattress in a courtyard house.
- **Downsides:** Fewer late-night options than Hongdae or Gangnam, and some hanok stays have small rooms and shared bathrooms. Bukchon is a residential area with visiting-hour rules for tourists, so keep noise down if you stay nearby.

**Choose it if:** your trip is about palaces, traditional culture and walking, and you prefer quiet evenings.

## Hongdae: nightlife and the easiest airport trip

The area around Hongik University is Seoul's youth and indie-music district, with busking on the streets, small bars, clubs, vintage shops and inexpensive restaurants. Neighboring **Yeonnam-dong** and **Mangwon-dong** are calmer, with cafés and the Gyeongui Line Forest Park walking trail.

- **Airport access:** Hongik Univ. Station is a stop on the **AREX All-Stop train**, so you can ride from Incheon Airport to Hongdae with no transfer for around ₩5,000 with a T-money card. Our [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](../incheon-airport-to-seoul/) compares the train, bus and taxi.
- **Getting around:** **Line 2** reaches Euljiro (for Myeongdong), Dongdaemun, Seongsu and Gangnam without changing trains. The palaces need one transfer.
- **Downsides:** Loud on Friday and Saturday nights near the main streets, and about 20 to 30 minutes by subway from the palaces.

**Choose it if:** you want nightlife, a younger atmosphere or good-value guesthouses, or you want the simplest airport transfer.

## Seoul Station: best for trains and early flights

Seoul Station is the city's main rail hub. The **AREX Express** runs non-stop from Incheon Airport, the **KTX** leaves from here for Busan and other cities, and **Lines 1 and 4** connect to the rest of the city.

- **Why stay here:** Ideal the night before an early KTX or flight, and handy if you plan several day trips. Our [Seoul to Busan KTX guide](../seoul-to-busan-ktx/) covers tickets and timing.
- **Downsides:** The area right around the station is mostly offices and roads, with less to do at night. Myeongdong is two stops away on Line 4, so many people stay in between.

**Choose it if:** your trip includes train travel, or you are in Seoul for just one or two nights.

## Dongdaemun: shopping late and good transfers

Dongdaemun is known for its fashion malls and the futuristic **Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP)**. Some wholesale and retail malls trade into the night, though opening hours vary by building and day, so check before you go.

- **Getting around:** **Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station** is served by **Lines 2, 4 and 5**, one of the best-connected stations for visitors. Line 4 goes straight to Myeongdong and Seoul Station.
- **Downsides:** Busy roads and a more commercial look; fewer cafés and quiet streets than Jongno or Hongdae.

**Choose it if:** you want a central, well-connected hotel and plan to shop.

## Gangnam: modern Seoul south of the river

Gangnam is Seoul's business and upscale shopping district, with wide avenues, office towers, COEX Mall and the Starfield Library, and many beauty and dermatology clinics.

- **Getting around:** Gangnam Station is on **Line 2** and the **Sinbundang Line**. Airport limousine buses serve major Gangnam hotels.
- **Downsides:** The palaces, Insadong and Myeongdong are on the other side of the river, typically 30 to 40 minutes away by subway with a transfer.

**Choose it if:** you have business in the area, appointments at a Gangnam clinic, or a K-pop itinerary focused south of the river.

## Itaewon and Seongsu: for second visits

**Itaewon (Line 6)** has Seoul's widest range of international restaurants, plus bars and Haebangchon's hillside streets nearby. It is a good choice for longer stays or if you want food from home. **Seongsu (Line 2)**, nicknamed Seoul's Brooklyn, is full of warehouse cafés, design shops and brand pop-up stores. Both are fun to stay in, but they are farther from the classic sights, so they suit repeat visitors best.

## Hotels, guesthouses and short-term rentals

Seoul has every kind of stay, from international chain hotels to compact Korean business hotels, hostels and hanok guesthouses.

- **Short-term rentals:** Airbnb began requiring Korean hosts to submit a business registration in 2024, and hosts who had not done so could not accept bookings for stays from **January 1, 2026**. As of October 2026, book only listings that look professionally registered, and read the cancellation policy carefully.
- **Check the nearest station and exit:** Listings often say "near Myeongdong" or "Hongdae area" when the property is a long walk away. Look up the walking time from the nearest station in a Korean map app. Our [Naver Map vs Kakao Map guide](../google-maps-korea-naver-kakao-map/) explains how.
- **Look for hills:** Parts of Myeongdong, Itaewon, Haebangchon and Bukchon are steep. If you have large suitcases, a hotel on flat ground next to a station saves effort.
- **Elevators at stations:** Not every station exit has an elevator or escalator. Map apps show which exits have elevators.

## FAQ

**What is the best area to stay in Seoul for a first-time visitor?**
Myeongdong or Jongno for most people. Both are central, close to the main sights, and have hotels in every price range. Hongdae is the best pick if nightlife and an easy airport train matter more to you.

**Is it better to stay in Gangnam or Myeongdong?**
For sightseeing, Myeongdong. Most classic attractions are north of the Han River, and Myeongdong is closer to them. Gangnam is better if your plans are mainly south of the river.

**Where should I stay in Seoul with kids?**
A hotel next to a subway station with an elevator exit, in a flatter area such as Jongno, Gwanghwamun, Seoul Station or Jamsil (near Lotte World), is usually easier than the steep streets of Myeongdong or Itaewon.

**Which area has the easiest airport access?**
Hongdae and Seoul Station, both on the AREX line from Incheon Airport. Myeongdong, Dongdaemun, Jongno and Gangnam have airport limousine bus stops close to many hotels.

**Do I need to stay near a subway station?**
Yes, it helps a lot. Seoul's subway is fast and cheap, and staying within a few minutes' walk of a station on Line 2, 3 or 4 puts most sights within easy reach. See our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/) for fares and T-money.

**Is it safe to stay anywhere in central Seoul?**
Seoul is generally considered a safe city for visitors, including at night in busy areas. Use normal precautions with belongings in crowds, especially in Myeongdong and Hongdae on weekends.
