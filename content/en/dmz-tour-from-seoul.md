The Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) that separates South and North Korea is one of the most popular day trips from Seoul. The main visitor area in Paju is only about 50 km north of the city, and you can see an infiltration tunnel dug toward Seoul, look across the border from an observatory, and walk around the Imjingak peace park in half a day.

> **Quick answer:** The easiest way is a **guided coach tour from Seoul**, which costs roughly **US$42–53 for a standard tour** (as of November 2026) and includes hotel-area pickup, the Third Tunnel, Dora Observatory and Imjingak. On a budget, you can go **on your own** to Imjingak by subway and bus, then join Paju City's **DMZ shuttle tour** for **₩9,200** (as of November 2026). Either way you need your **original passport**. The **JSA (Panmunjom)**, where the blue huts are, has been **closed to tourists since July 2023** and was still closed in November 2026.

## Is the JSA (Panmunjom) open?

No. Civilian tours of the Joint Security Area at Panmunjom were suspended on July 18, 2023, after a US soldier on a tour ran across the border into North Korea. As of November 2026, the Ministry of Unification's Panmunjom tour booking calendar shows no tours, and it says any restart will be announced there.

Be careful when booking: in 2026, tours sold as "DMZ + JSA" usually mean a visit to a **JSA-related museum or exhibition**, not Panmunjom itself. Read the itinerary before you pay. If a site still describes visiting the blue conference rooms or standing at the military demarcation line, that information is out of date.

What you **can** visit is the Paju DMZ area just south of the border, which is what almost every tour from Seoul covers.

## What you see on a DMZ tour

| Stop | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Imjingak Peace Park | Park by the Imjin River with the Freedom Bridge, a bullet-scarred steam locomotive and memorials to families separated by the war | Free to walk around, outside the controlled zone |
| Third Infiltration Tunnel | A tunnel dug from the North toward Seoul, found in 1978; about 1,635 m long and 73 m underground at its deepest | Steep slope down; the highlight of most tours |
| Dora Observatory | Viewing hall with telescopes looking across to North Korea, including the town of Kaesong on clear days | Visibility depends on weather and haze |
| Dorasan Station | The last station on the line north, built for a future rail link to the North | Included on many coach tours and on the special DMZ train |
| Tongilchon (Unification Village) | A farming village inside the civilian control zone | Lunch or shopping stop on some tours |
| Imjingak Peace Gondola | Cable car across the Imjin River into the civilian control zone | Optional add-on, separate ticket |

Some tours also add a suspension bridge (such as Gamaksan or Majang Lake) or a talk by a North Korean defector.

## Option 1: Guided tour from Seoul

This is what most visitors do. A coach picks you up in central Seoul (often near City Hall, Myeongdong or Hongdae), the guide handles the paperwork and tickets for the controlled zone, and you get history and context in English along the way. The drive takes about an hour each way.

| Tour type | Typical length | Typical price (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard DMZ tour (Imjingak, Third Tunnel, Dora Observatory) | Half day, about 6 hours | From about US$42–53 |
| Tour with defector talk or extra stops | 6–9 hours | From about US$50 |
| Small-group or Cheorwon DMZ tour with lunch | Full day | From about US$120 |

Tips for booking:

- **Book at least a few days ahead**, and earlier for weekends. Operators need your passport details in advance for the controlled zone.
- Check whether the price includes **admission fees, lunch and the gondola**. Cheaper tours often skip lunch.
- Check whether the tour **goes into the Third Tunnel**. A few very short tours only visit Imjingak.
- Tours do not run on **Mondays** because the controlled sites are closed (see below).

## Option 2: On your own with the Paju DMZ shuttle tour

If you don't mind no guide, Paju City runs its own **DMZ Peace Tour** bus from the Imjingak ticket office. It is the cheapest way in and foreigners can join as individuals.

**Step 1. Get to Imjingak.** Take the **Gyeongui-Jungang Line** (it stops at Seoul Station, Hongik University and other central stations) to **Munsan Station**, the end of most trains. From Munsan take **village bus 058** (daily) or **bus 93-9** (weekends) to the Imjingak stop. A few trains continue one stop to **Imjingang Station**, about an 8-minute walk from the park. Plan on roughly 1.5 hours from central Seoul and use Naver Map or Kakao Map for live times.

**Step 2. Buy a ticket at the Imjingak ticket office.** Tickets are issued from 09:00 to 14:30. Every person must show an **original passport** in person, and tickets are only issued when the whole group is there. Some seats on each bus can be reserved online on Paju City's DMZ site (dmz.paju.go.kr, Korean); the rest are sold at the window on a first-come basis. On weekends the morning buses can sell out, so arrive early.

**Step 3. Ride the shuttle.** The bus takes you through the military checkpoint to the Third Tunnel, Dora Observatory and back. The whole trip takes about **3 hours**, and buses leave at intervals from about 09:20 until mid-afternoon.

| Paju DMZ shuttle tour (as of November 2026) | Adult | Youth | Senior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking route into the tunnel | ₩9,200 | ₩7,000 | ₩6,200 |
| Monorail route into the tunnel | ₩12,200 | ₩9,500 | ₩7,700 |

Foreign visitors pay the standard fares. The **monorail has been listed as "under inspection"** on some days in 2026, with every departure sold as the walking route, so don't count on it.

**Closed:** every **Monday**, weekday public holidays, and the Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok holidays. Weekends are open.

## Imjingak Peace Gondola

The gondola crosses about 850 m over the Imjin River to a small area inside the civilian control zone with an observation deck and exhibits. The ride is about 10 minutes each way.

As of November 2026, a standard-cabin round trip is **₩12,000 for adults** and **₩10,000 for children**, and a glass-floor crystal cabin is ₩15,000 for adults. Hours are 09:00–18:00 on weekdays and 09:00–19:00 on weekends and holidays, with the last tickets 30 minutes before closing. You need ID (a passport is fine). Prices on other pages differ slightly, so check the official DMZ Gondola site before you go.

## Rules you need to know

- **Bring your original passport.** Photos, copies and screenshots are refused, and so is a passport whose permitted stay has expired.
- **Photography is restricted.** On Paju City's official course, photos and video are banned at controlled sites, and cameras are not allowed inside the Third Tunnel. Drones and action cameras are banned. Follow your guide and the signs.
- **Stay on marked paths.** The area near the border may contain mines.
- **Wear sturdy shoes.** The walk down into the Third Tunnel is a steep, about 11-degree slope, and the climb back up is tiring.
- **Health limits.** People with heart or blood pressure problems, breathing difficulties or limited mobility, and wheelchair users may be advised not to enter the tunnel. You can wait at the top.
- **Children** can join with a guardian.
- **Schedules can change.** Tours can be cancelled at short notice for military or security reasons.

## When to go

The DMZ is open all year except Mondays and major holidays. **Weekdays** are quieter. In winter (December to February) Paju is colder than Seoul and the riverside is windy, so dress warmly; temperatures often fall well below freezing in the morning. Haze can limit the view from Dora Observatory, so clear days after rain are best.

There is also a special **DMZ Peace-Link train** from Seoul Station to Dorasan, which restarted in 2026 after a long pause. It runs only on a few days a month and is booked as a package through Korail, so it is not a practical choice for most short trips.

## FAQ

**Can I visit the DMZ without a tour?**
Yes. Take the subway and bus to Imjingak, then buy a ticket for Paju City's DMZ shuttle tour with your passport. Imjingak itself and the gondola need no tour.

**Is the JSA open to tourists in 2026?**
No. JSA (Panmunjom) tours have been suspended since July 2023 and were still suspended as of November 2026.

**How much does a DMZ tour from Seoul cost?**
As of November 2026, standard tours start around US$42–53. The Paju shuttle tour from Imjingak is ₩9,200 for the walking route.

**Do I need my passport for the DMZ?**
Yes, the original. Tour operators also ask for your passport details when you book.

**Is the DMZ open on Mondays?**
No. The Third Tunnel, Dora Observatory and the Paju shuttle tour are closed on Mondays, so tours from Seoul don't run that day.

**Is it safe to visit the DMZ?**
The visitor areas in Paju are run as tourist sites and receive large numbers of visitors. Follow the rules, stay on marked paths and expect closures if tensions rise.

**How long does a DMZ tour take?**
A standard tour from Seoul takes about 6 hours, including about an hour's drive each way. The Paju shuttle tour from Imjingak takes about 3 hours.
