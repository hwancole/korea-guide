Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) is the largest of Seoul's five Joseon-era royal palaces and the one most first-time visitors see. It sits at the north end of Gwanghwamun Square, with Bugaksan mountain behind it, and is easy to reach by subway.

> **Quick answer:** Adult admission is **3,000 won** (ages 19 to 64), and entry is **free if you wear hanbok** with a proper top and bottom. The palace opens at **9:00 a.m.** and closes at **5:00 p.m. in November to February**, 6:00 p.m. in spring and autumn, and 6:30 p.m. in summer, with last entry one hour before closing. It is **closed on Tuesdays**. Get off at **Gyeongbokgung Station (Line 3), Exit 5**, or Gwanghwamun Station (Line 5), Exit 2. Plan on 2 to 3 hours, and arrive by about 9:45 a.m. to also catch the morning guard ceremony.

Fees and hours below are as of November 2026, from Seoul's official tourism site (Visit Seoul) and recent reporting. Palace rules can change, so check again close to your visit.

## Tickets and who gets in free

| Visitor | Price (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|
| Adults, 19 to 64 | 3,000 won |
| Ages 7 to 18 | 1,500 won |
| Age 6 and under | Free |
| Age 65 and over | Free (bring ID) |
| Anyone wearing hanbok | Free |
| Everyone, on "Culture Day" (last Wednesday of the month) | Free |

You buy tickets at the ticket booths just inside Gwanghwamun Gate, before Heungnyemun, the second gate. Cards are accepted, so you do not need cash (our [guide to paying in Korea](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/) covers which cards work).

The next Culture Days are **November 25** and **December 30, 2026**. Expect bigger crowds on those days.

### The combined palace ticket

If you plan to see more than one palace, the **Integrated Palace Ticket** costs **6,000 won** and gives one entry each to Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung (palace grounds only), Changgyeonggung, Deoksugung and Jongmyo Shrine, valid for six months from purchase. Bought separately, those five cost 9,000 won. It does **not** include the Huwon (Secret Garden) tour at Changdeokgung, which needs its own timed ticket. You buy it at the ticket office of any included site. These details are from an October 2026 update; confirm at the booth.

If you are wearing hanbok, you get into all of these for free anyway, so the pass only makes sense in regular clothes.

The palace is **closed every Tuesday**. If a public holiday falls on a Tuesday, it opens that day and closes on the next non-holiday day instead. Hours are as of November 2026.

Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung close on Mondays instead, so if you are planning several palaces, Monday is a good day for Gyeongbokgung and Tuesday for the others.

## Free entry in hanbok: what counts

Since 2013, anyone in hanbok, Korean or foreign, gets into the main palaces and Jongmyo for free. Rental shops line the streets near Gyeongbokgung Station Exits 4 and 5, and most rent by the hour, so you can change, walk over and enter without a ticket.

Under the guidance reported in spring 2026, the outfit has to be recognisably hanbok:

- **A top and a bottom.** The top should be a jeogori (the short jacket) with the crossed, overlapping collar, and the bottom a hanbok skirt or hanbok trousers.
- **Modern and fusion hanbok are fine**, including the shorter rental styles. The ties (goreum) do not have to be tied in a particular way.
- **Any gender can wear any style.** Men in skirts and women in trousers are admitted; the old gender rule was dropped in 2019.
- **Likely to be refused:** a jeogori over jeans, a hanbok skirt with a plain T-shirt, a one-piece robe (such as a cheollik or king's robe) worn over normal clothes, or a costume the staff consider unsuitable for the palace.

Enforcement at the gate has been reported as uneven, and the palace office has said it may revise the rules, so if your outfit is unusual, carry a little cash or a card in case you are asked to buy a ticket. Our [winter clothing guide](../korea-winter-weather-what-to-wear/) is worth a look if you visit from December: rental hanbok is thin, and you will want a thermal layer underneath.

## Royal guard changing ceremony

The guard changing ceremony (수문장 교대의식) recreates the Joseon-era change of the palace gate guards, with drums, flags and costumed guards.

| What | Time | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Guard changing ceremony | 10:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m., about 20 minutes | Between Gwanghwamun and Heungnyemun gates |
| Public guard training | 9:35 a.m. and 1:35 p.m., about 15 minutes | Outside Hyeopsaengmun Gate (east side) |

It is held every day **except Tuesdays**, and can be shortened or cancelled in heavy rain or extreme heat or cold. You do not need a ticket to watch, because it takes place in front of the ticket gate. For a spot at the front, arrive 10 to 15 minutes early. Times are as reported in October 2026; check on the day if the weather is bad.

## Free guided tours in English

Free English tours run daily except Tuesdays at **11:00 a.m., 1:30 p.m. and 3:30 p.m.**, as of November 2026. They are included in your admission (or hanbok entry). Small groups and individuals simply join; groups of 10 or more must book in advance. Ask at the information desk near Heungnyemun Gate for the meeting point on the day.

A tour is the easiest way to understand what you are looking at. Without one, most of the halls are just beautiful buildings.

## What to see, in order

Walking north from the main gate, a simple route takes about two hours:

1. **Gwanghwamun Gate** and the guard ceremony.
2. **Heungnyemun Gate**, then **Geunjeongjeon**, the throne hall, with its stone courtyard and rank markers.
3. **Gyeonghoeru Pavilion**, the large pavilion on a pond, the classic photo spot, to the west of the throne hall.
4. **Gangnyeongjeon and Gyotaejeon**, the king's and queen's living quarters, and the **Amisan garden** behind them.
5. **Hyangwonjeong Pavilion**, a small hexagonal pavilion on an island at the north end, especially pretty in autumn and after snow.
6. Leave by the east side for the **National Folk Museum of Korea**, or by the west side toward the **National Palace Museum** near the main gate.

From the east exit, it is a short walk to Samcheong-dong and Bukchon Hanok Village, which makes an easy half-day together.

## Night openings

Gyeongbokgung opens for a limited number of evenings in spring and autumn, with online tickets that sell out quickly. In 2026, the autumn session ran from September 2 to October 2 and has finished. Look for the spring 2027 dates to be announced in early spring; booking is usually through the NOL (formerly Interpark) ticket site.

## Tips for a smoother visit

- **Go early.** At 9:00 a.m. the courtyards are quiet; by late morning, tour groups and hanbok photo shoots fill Gyeonghoeru.
- **Allow time for the walk.** The grounds are large and mostly gravel and stone. Wear comfortable shoes.
- **There is little shade or indoor space** in the main courtyards, so bring water in summer and warm layers in winter.
- **Pair it with lunch nearby.** Seochon to the west and Samcheong-dong to the east are both a short walk and full of small restaurants and cafes.

## FAQ

**Is Gyeongbokgung open on Mondays?**
Yes. It is closed on Tuesdays, not Mondays, as of November 2026.

**How much is the Gyeongbokgung ticket for foreigners?**
The same as for Koreans: 3,000 won for adults aged 19 to 64, 1,500 won for ages 7 to 18, and free for children 6 and under and adults 65 and over.

**Do I really get in free if I rent a hanbok?**
Yes, as long as it is a hanbok top and bottom. Typical rental hanbok, including modern styles, is accepted. A hanbok top with jeans, or a robe over everyday clothes, may not be.

**Do I need to book tickets in advance?**
No, daytime tickets are sold at the gate. Only the seasonal night openings need advance online booking.

**What time is the guard changing ceremony?**
At 10:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m., except Tuesdays, as of late 2026. It lasts about 20 minutes and is free to watch.

**How long does a visit take?**
About 2 hours for the main halls, or 3 hours with a guided tour or one of the museums.

**Is the Integrated Palace Ticket worth it?**
Only if you will visit at least three of the included sites in regular clothes. At 6,000 won for five sites (9,000 won separately), it pays off quickly, but it does not cover Huwon.
