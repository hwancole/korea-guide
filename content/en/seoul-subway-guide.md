Seoul's subway is one of the easiest big-city systems in the world to use: signs are in English, Chinese and Japanese, trains run every few minutes, and a single card pays for subways, city buses and even many taxis. The only things that trip visitors up are the payment options and a few local habits. This guide covers both.

> **Quick answer:** Buy a T-money card at any convenience store (around ₩3,000–4,000), load it with cash, and tap in and out. A basic subway ride costs **₩1,550** with a card. If you'll ride more than about 3–4 times a day inside Seoul, look at the **Climate Card** tourist pass instead.

## How much does the Seoul subway cost?

Fares are distance-based. The adult base fare covers your first 10 km, which is enough for most trips inside central Seoul.

| Ride | Adult fare (with a transit card) |
|---|---|
| Subway, first 10 km | ₩1,550 |
| Subway, longer trips | ₩100 more per extra 5 km (then per 8 km beyond 50 km) |
| Blue or green city bus | ₩1,500 |
| Single-journey paper/plastic ticket | Card fare + ₩100, plus a ₩500 deposit |

The base fare went up to ₩1,550 in mid-2025, so older blog posts that say ₩1,250 or ₩1,400 are out of date. Fares can change again, so treat these as a guide and check the station machine screen if you're unsure.

## Option 1: T-money card (best for most visitors)

T-money is a rechargeable transit card, a bit like London's Oyster or Japan's Suica. It works on the subway and buses across Seoul and most other Korean cities, and in many taxis and convenience stores.

**Where to buy it:** Any CU, GS25, 7-Eleven or Emart24 convenience store, including the ones in the Incheon and Gimpo airport arrival halls. Ask for "T-money card". The card itself costs roughly ₩3,000–4,000 depending on the design, and that price is not refunded.

**How to top it up:**

1. At a subway station, find the ticket machine and switch the screen to English.
2. Place the card on the reader, choose "Transportation Card Top-up" and the amount.
3. Insert **cash** (₩1,000, ₩5,000, ₩10,000 or ₩50,000 notes).

You can also ask the cashier at any convenience store to charge it. In both cases plan on paying with cash; foreign credit cards generally can't be used to load T-money.

**Getting leftover money back:** Small balances (up to ₩20,000) can be refunded at major convenience stores for a ₩500 fee. Larger balances go through a T-money service center. Many visitors simply keep the card for their next trip, since the balance doesn't expire quickly.

## Option 2: Climate Card tourist pass (best for heavy riders)

The Climate Card (기후동행카드) is Seoul's unlimited-ride pass. The short-term versions for visitors cost:

| Pass | Price |
|---|---|
| 1 day | ₩5,000 |
| 2 days | ₩8,000 |
| 3 days | ₩10,000 |
| 5 days | ₩15,000 |
| 7 days | ₩20,000 |

You also need the physical card, which is about ₩3,000. Buy and load it at the customer service centers in major stations; convenience stores sell the card but often can't load the tourist pass.

**Is it worth it?** A 3-day pass plus the card costs about ₩13,000, which equals roughly 8 subway rides. If you plan around 3 or more rides a day, it pays off. If you'll mostly walk around one neighborhood, a T-money card is cheaper.

**The catch:** It's a Seoul pass. It doesn't cover everything, notably the express train to Incheon Airport and most trips out into Gyeonggi province. Check the coverage on the Seoul city website before relying on it for an airport run or a day trip.

## Free transfers: the rule that saves you money

When you pay with a card, transfers between subway and bus (or bus to bus) are free or nearly free if you board within **30 minutes** of getting off. Late at night (9 PM–7 AM) the window stretches to 60 minutes. You only pay extra if the total distance is long.

The one thing you must do: **tap your card when you get off the bus**, not just when you get on. If you forget, the system can't link the trips and you'll pay a new base fare on the next ride.

## Step by step: your first ride

1. **Find the station.** Look for the line number and color on the sign at the entrance.
2. **Tap in** at the gate with your card. You'll hear a beep and see your balance.
3. **Check the direction.** Platforms are labeled with the next station and the end of the line, e.g. "Line 2 → City Hall".
4. **Ride.** Announcements are made in Korean and English, and screens show the next station.
5. **Tap out** at the exit gate.
6. **Pick the right exit.** Stations have numbered exits (Exit 1, Exit 2, ...) that can be several minutes apart. Map apps tell you which exit is closest to your destination, so check it before you leave the platform.

## Apps that actually work in Korea

- **Naver Map** and **KakaoMap** are what locals use. Both have English menus and show real-time train and bus arrivals, the best car to board for a quick transfer, and exit numbers.
- **Google Maps** shows subway routes, but walking and driving directions are limited in Korea, so it's best as a backup.
- **Subway Korea** (or any Korean subway app) is handy for an offline line map.

## Local etiquette worth knowing

- **Pink seats** in the middle of each car are reserved for pregnant women. Leave them empty even when the train is crowded.
- **Priority seats** at the ends of each car are for seniors and people with disabilities.
- Keep phone calls short and quiet; most people ride in silence.
- During rush hour (about 7:30–9:00 and 18:00–19:30), Line 2 and the Line 9 express trains get very crowded. Travel a little earlier or later if you have luggage.
- Most lines stop running around midnight, so check the last train time in your map app on late nights.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I pay with my phone or a foreign credit card at the gate?** Some newer systems accept contactless foreign cards on selected routes, but it isn't universal yet. A T-money or Climate Card works everywhere, so it's still the safe choice.

**Do kids pay less?** Yes, children and teens get discounted fares, but their card must be registered as a child or youth card at a station or convenience store, otherwise it charges the adult fare.

**Does T-money work outside Seoul?** Yes, in Busan, Daegu, Incheon, Jeju buses and most other cities. The Climate Card does not.
