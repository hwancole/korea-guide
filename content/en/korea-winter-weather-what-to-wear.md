Korean winters are cold, dry and mostly sunny. Seoul is much colder than Tokyo, London or Paris at the same time of year, and the wind off the Han River makes it feel colder still. Snow falls on a handful of days each month but rarely piles up in the city. The south coast (Busan) and Jeju island are noticeably milder.

> **Quick answer:** In Seoul from December to February, daytime highs average about **2–5°C** and nights average about **−3 to −6°C**, with cold snaps that can drop well below −10°C. Pack a **warm padded coat (ideally knee-length)**, a thermal base layer, a hat, scarf and gloves, and **warm, grippy shoes**. Dress in layers you can remove, because shops, subways and restaurants are well heated. Busan and Jeju are around 5–8°C warmer, so a mid-weight coat is usually enough there.

Figures below are long-term averages from the Korea Meteorological Administration (1991–2020 climate normals). Actual weather varies from year to year, so check the forecast a few days before you fly.

*Source: Korea Meteorological Administration climate normals (1991–2020), as compiled for each city. "Days with snow" counts any day snow was observed, including light flurries that do not settle.*

A few things the averages do not show:

- **Cold snaps.** Every winter brings spells of Siberian air when Seoul stays below freezing all day and mornings reach −10°C to −15°C. Seoul's record low is below −20°C. These spells often last a few days and then ease, a pattern Koreans describe as *samhan saon* ("three cold days, four warm days").
- **Wind chill.** A breezy −5°C morning by the river or on a mountain viewpoint like Namsan can feel closer to −12°C.
- **Dry air.** Winter is the driest season in Seoul, and the air indoors is drier still because of heating. Lips, hands and skin dry out fast.
- **Short days.** In Seoul the sun rises around **7:30–7:45** and sets around **17:15** in December, so plan outdoor sightseeing for the middle of the day. By mid-February, sunset moves to about 18:10.

## Month by month

### December

Early December still feels like late autumn on mild days, but by mid-month freezing nights are normal. It is a good month for Christmas lights in central Seoul and department-store displays. Rain is rare, and most days are clear. Late December is usually when the first serious cold spell arrives.

### January

The coldest month. Seoul's average daily high is just above freezing, and many rivers and streams outside the city freeze over. This is peak season for ski resorts in Gangwon Province and for winter festivals such as the Hwacheon Sancheoneo Ice Festival, which is usually held in January (check the official dates for the year you travel). If you hate the cold, January is the month to base yourself in Busan or Jeju.

### February

Still cold, especially early in the month, but days get longer and milder toward the end. Lunar New Year (Seollal) falls in late January or February, and in 2027 the holiday falls in early February. Many shops and restaurants close for a day or two around Seollal, and trains and buses fill up as people travel to their hometowns, so book transport early if your trip overlaps.

## What to wear: a winter packing list for Seoul

The key is **one serious outer layer plus thin layers underneath**. You will move constantly between freezing streets and warm subways, cafés and shops, and a heavy jumper under your coat quickly becomes too hot indoors.

| Layer | What to bring | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Outer | Padded or down coat, knee-length if possible; windproof | Long padded coats are what most Seoul residents wear in January for good reason: they block wind on your legs |
| Mid | Fleece, thin knit or light down vest | Easy to remove indoors |
| Base | Thermal top and leggings (merino or synthetic) | Keeps you warm without bulk; leggings under jeans make a big difference |
| Head and hands | Beanie, scarf or neck warmer, touchscreen gloves | Ears and fingers get cold first; gloves that work with phones help with maps |
| Feet | Water-resistant shoes or boots with grippy soles, warm socks | Sidewalks and stairs can be icy after snow; thin canvas sneakers get cold and wet |
| Extras | Lip balm, rich hand cream, moisturizer | The air is very dry |

**For Busan and Jeju:** a mid-weight coat or a padded jacket with a sweater is usually enough on most days. Jeju is windy and sees frequent light rain or snow showers in winter, so bring a waterproof or windproof outer layer. If you plan to climb Hallasan in winter, conditions on the mountain are far harsher than on the coast and require proper winter hiking gear, including crampons.

**If you forget something:** You do not need to bring everything from home. Seoul is an easy place to buy winter clothes at reasonable prices:

- **Convenience stores** (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven) sell disposable heat packs, gloves, socks and lip balm.
- **Underground shopping arcades** at stations such as Express Bus Terminal (Goto Mall) and Gangnam sell inexpensive scarves, hats and knitwear.
- **Department stores and outdoor brands** stock serious padded coats, and many shops offer instant tax refunds to foreign visitors.

## Tips for staying comfortable

1. **Use heat packs.** Disposable heat packs, sold in convenience stores and pharmacies, are widely used in Korea. The stick-on type goes inside a jacket or on your lower back; others go in your pockets or shoes.
2. **Dress for the heating.** Korean homes and many guesthouses use *ondol*, underfloor heating that can make rooms very warm and very dry. Hotel rooms are often warm too. Sleep with a glass of water nearby, or ask whether a humidifier is available.
3. **Check the air quality.** Fine dust (PM2.5 and PM10) tends to be worse in winter and early spring. The Korean government runs a seasonal fine dust management period from December through March. Check the AirKorea app or your weather app before a long day outdoors, and wear a KF94 mask on high-dust days if you are sensitive.
4. **Watch for ice.** After snow, side streets, stairs and steep alleys in neighborhoods like Bukchon and Ihwa can be slippery for a few days. Walk carefully and wear shoes with grip.
5. **Plan indoor backups.** Museums, palaces with indoor exhibits, covered markets, underground malls connected to subway stations, and saunas (*jjimjilbang*) are good options on bitter days.
6. **Charge your phone.** Phone batteries drain faster in the cold. A small power bank in an inside pocket helps if you rely on your phone for maps and payments.

## Is winter a good time to visit Korea?

Winter has real advantages: clear skies, fewer crowds at major sights than in spring cherry blossom season or autumn foliage season, and seasonal food like hotteok (sweet filled pancakes), bungeoppang (fish-shaped pastries) and steaming bowls of tteokguk and stews. It is also ski season. The trade-offs are the cold, short days, and the chance of a few high fine-dust days. If you pack properly, Seoul in winter is very manageable.

## FAQ

**Does it snow in Seoul?**
Yes, but usually lightly. Seoul averages about five to seven days with some snow in each of December, January and February. Heavy snowfalls that settle for days are uncommon but do happen a few times a winter. If snow is a priority, the mountains of Gangwon Province are a far safer bet.

**Is Seoul colder than Tokyo in winter?**
Yes, by several degrees. Seoul's January average high is about 2°C, while Tokyo's is closer to 10°C. Seoul is also windier and drier, so dress as you would for a cold continental city.

**Do I need a long padded coat?**
Not strictly, but it is the single most useful item for Seoul in January. A hip-length down jacket works fine if you add thermal leggings and stay out of the wind.

**What should I wear for Christmas or New Year's Eve in Seoul?**
Plan for freezing temperatures after dark, especially if you will be standing outside for a countdown or watching lights. Add an extra layer, a hat and gloves, and bring heat packs.

**Is it worth going to Busan or Jeju to escape the cold?**
They are milder, with January highs around 8–9°C, but they are not warm. Jeju is also windier and rainier in winter than Seoul. Expect cool rather than cold weather, and still pack a proper jacket.

**Will my flights or trains be disrupted by snow?**
Disruptions are occasional rather than common. Heavy snow can delay flights at Incheon and Jeju, and Jeju flights are sometimes affected by strong winds. Keep some flexibility in your schedule and check your airline's notices on bad-weather days.
