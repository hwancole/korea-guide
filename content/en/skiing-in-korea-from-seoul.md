Korea is an easy place to try skiing or snowboarding on a winter trip. Several resorts sit within one to two hours of Seoul, slopes are groomed and floodlit for night skiing, and you can rent everything, including jackets and trousers, at the base. The snow is mostly machine-made and the runs are shorter than in Japan or the Alps, so think of it as a fun day out rather than a powder trip.

> **Quick answer:** For a **day trip from Seoul**, choose **Vivaldi Park** (shuttle buses from Hongdae and Myeongdong, big beginner area) or **Elysian Gangchon** (reachable by train, then a short walk). For **bigger mountains**, stay one or two nights at **Mona Yongpyong** or **High1** in Gangwon Province. Most resorts open between **late November and mid-December** and close in **March**. Expect roughly **₩80,000–105,000** for a day lift ticket and **₩35,000–50,000** for a ski or board rental set, based on the 2025-26 price lists (as of November 2026).

Opening dates and prices below are as of November 2026. Most resorts publish their 2026-27 price lists only a few weeks before opening, so check the resort site before you go.

## When does the 2026-27 ski season start?

As of early November 2026, no major resort has announced an exact opening date, but snowmaking has already begun. **Mona Yongpyong** started making snow on October 22 and is aiming for a late-November opening, and **High1** began on October 28, also targeting late November. **Phoenix Pyeongchang** has told the press it plans to open on **November 27, 2026**, weather permitting, and run to mid-March 2027.

On opening day only one or two slopes are usually ready, and more open over the following weeks. **The best snow coverage and the most open slopes are usually from late December to mid-February.** Weekends and the Korean school winter holidays (roughly January to February) are busiest, so ski on a weekday if you can.

## Lift tickets, rentals and lessons

Korean resorts sell lift tickets by the **number of hours** (for example 2, 4, 5 or 7 hours) or by session (day, evening, late night), not just by the day. Weekend and holiday prices are higher than weekday prices.

Typical prices from last season's (2025-26) lists, as of November 2026:

**Money-saving tips:**

- **Book online.** Most resorts charge less on their website or app than at the ticket window, and travel platforms such as Klook, Trazy and KKday sell bundles of shuttle bus, lift ticket and rental, which are often cheaper than buying each part separately.
- **Compare the English and Korean pages.** Some resorts list different products and prices for foreign visitors. In September 2026, for example, Konjiam's English page showed lower lift prices than its Korean page, and Konjiam does not sell lift tickets at an on-site window, so you must buy online.
- **Ski a short session.** If you are a beginner, a 3- or 4-hour ticket is usually plenty for the first day.

**Rentals:** You can rent skis or a snowboard, boots, helmet, goggles and clothing at the resort or at rental shops on the access roads. Rental desks usually ask for an ID, so bring your **passport**. Gloves are often sold rather than rented, so bring your own. Helmets are increasingly required; Vivaldi Park made them mandatory on its slopes last season.

**Lessons:** Several resorts offer group lessons in English. Mona Yongpyong's 2025-26 group lesson in English cost ₩70,000 per person (minimum four people). Private lessons cost more, and some resorts charge instructors a separate lesson permit fee, so ask what is included before you pay.

## Getting there without a car

### Shuttle buses and day tours (easiest)

The simplest option is a **round-trip shuttle bus** or a **day tour** booked online. These run daily from about December to early March.

- **Vivaldi Park shuttle (sold on Trazy):** departs at **7:00 from Hongik University Station Exit 8** and **7:30 from Euljiro 1-ga Station Exit 8** (near Myeongdong), and leaves the resort at **18:00**. Book by 16:00 Korea time the day before. The bus does not wait for latecomers.
- **Tour packages** to Vivaldi Park, Elysian Gangchon or Mona Yongpyong typically leave Hongdae, Myeongdong or Dongdaemun between about 6:40 and 8:00 and often include a lift ticket, equipment and clothing. They suit first-timers because everything is arranged.
- **Elysian Gangchon** also runs free shuttle buses from several points around Seoul in season. Check the resort's notices for the 2026-27 routes and how to reserve.

### By train

- **Elysian Gangchon:** Take the **Gyeongchun Line** (or the faster ITX-Cheongchun) toward Chuncheon and get off at **Baegyang-ri Station**. The slopes are a short walk or free shuttle ride from the station, and the fare is only a few thousand won.
- **Konjiam:** Take the **Gyeonggang Line** from Pangyo (connected to the Shinbundang Line from Gangnam) to **Gonjiam Station**, then a taxi of about 10 minutes.
- **Mona Yongpyong and Alpensia:** Take the **KTX** from Seoul or Cheongnyangni to **Jinbu Station**. Yongpyong's free Jinbu shuttle runs only a few times a day and is reserved for resort guests, who must show a booking, so day visitors usually take a taxi (about 20 minutes).
- **High1:** Trains on the Taebaek Line stop at **Gohan** and **Sabuk** stations, from which you continue to the resort by taxi or, where offered, the resort's shuttle; tour buses from Seoul are the simpler option.

Book KTX and ITX seats on the Korail website or app in advance for weekends, as ski-season trains fill up. For the subway leg, see our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/).

## Night skiing

Night skiing is a big part of Korean ski culture, and it is a good way to avoid daytime crowds. In 2025-26, Vivaldi Park ran slopes from 9:00 to 23:00 daily and added a late-night session to 3:00 between mid-December and mid-February (except Mondays). Elysian Gangchon and Jisan also run night and late-night sessions until about 2:00. Evening lift tickets are cheaper than day tickets. Temperatures after dark often fall well below freezing, so read our guide to [Korea's winter weather and what to wear](../korea-winter-weather-what-to-wear/) before you pack.

## Tips for your first Korean ski day

- **Leave early or go on a weekday.** Rental queues at big resorts can be long on weekend mornings.
- **Rent clothing if you only ski once.** A rented jacket and trousers cost far less than buying a full outfit for one day.
- **Bring a card and a little cash:** Most resort shops and restaurants accept foreign cards, but a little cash helps at small rental shops. See [paying in Korea](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/).
- **Ski etiquette:** Beginners should stay on slopes marked for beginners on the resort map and stop at the side of the run, not in the middle.
- **Travel insurance:** Check that your policy covers skiing and snowboarding.

## FAQ

**Which ski resort is closest to Seoul?**
Konjiam Resort in Gwangju, Gyeonggi Province, is the closest, about 40 minutes by rail from Gangnam plus a short taxi ride. Jisan Forest Resort in Icheon is also close.

**What is the best ski resort in Korea for beginners?**
Vivaldi Park and Elysian Gangchon are popular with first-timers because they are close to Seoul and have dedicated beginner slopes. Mona Yongpyong and High1 have beginner areas too, but they are better value on a stay of a night or more.

**Can I do a ski day trip from Seoul?**
Yes. Vivaldi Park, Elysian Gangchon, Jisan and Konjiam all work as day trips. Mona Yongpyong is possible on an early tour bus, but it is a long day.

**When is the best time to ski in Korea?**
From late December to mid-February, when most slopes are open. The season usually runs from late November or early December to March.

**How much does a day of skiing in Korea cost?**
Using last season's prices, plan on roughly ₩80,000–105,000 for a lift ticket, ₩35,000–50,000 for equipment, plus clothing rental and transport. Bundles from booking platforms can bring the total down.

**Do I need to bring ski clothes?**
No. Jackets, trousers, helmets and goggles can all be rented. Bring gloves, warm base layers and a neck warmer.

**Is the snow good in Korea?**
Snow is mostly machine-made and firm, and it is cold enough to stay in good condition in mid-winter. Gangwon resorts such as Mona Yongpyong at higher altitude usually have the most reliable conditions.
