Taxis in Korea are plentiful, metered and cheap compared with Tokyo, London or New York, and drivers do not expect a tip. The only real hurdle for visitors is language and payment, and an app solves both.

> **Quick answer:** As of November 2026, a regular Seoul taxi starts at **4,800 won for the first 1.6 km** (4 a.m. to 10 p.m.), with higher late-night rates from 10 p.m. to 4 a.m. You can hail one on the street when the windshield sign shows a red **빈차** (vacant), and pay by card, cash or T-money. To book from your phone, the easiest options for visitors are **k.ride** (Kakao Mobility's app for foreigners, foreign cards, no Kakao account needed, 10% service fee) and **Uber**, which books licensed taxis in Seoul. **Kakao T** is the app locals use; it takes foreign Visa, Mastercard, JCB and Amex cards for taxis, but sign-up needs phone verification that may not work with every foreign number.

Fares and app rules below are as of November 2026 and are Seoul's; other cities have similar but slightly different fares.

## Seoul taxi fares in 2026

Seoul has three main meter types. The regular (mid-size) taxi is the one you will use most.

| Taxi type | Base fare (daytime) | Then | Late night |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular (mid-size, orange, white or silver) | 4,800 won for the first 1.6 km | 100 won per 131 m, or per 30 seconds when moving slowly | Higher rates 10 p.m. to 4 a.m. (see below) |
| Deluxe (모범, mobeom, black) | 7,000 won for the first 3 km | 200 won per 151 m or per 36 seconds | 20% more, 10 p.m. to 4 a.m. |
| Large (대형, daehyeong, vans for up to about 8 people) | Same as deluxe | Same as deluxe | 20% more, 10 p.m. to 4 a.m. |

**Late-night regular fares.** The meter switches to a higher rate automatically:

| Time | Base fare | Increment |
|---|---|---|
| 4 a.m. to 10 p.m. | 4,800 won | 100 won |
| 10 to 11 p.m. and 2 to 4 a.m. (about 20% more) | 5,800 won | 120 won |
| 11 p.m. to 2 a.m. (about 40% more) | 6,700 won | 140 won |

These late-night rates already include the surcharge, so a driver should not add 20% or 40% on top.

**Other charges to know:**

- **Out-of-city surcharge:** a 20% surcharge can apply on eligible trips that leave Seoul's service area. Combined with the late-night surcharge, the total is capped at 60% for regular taxis.
- **Incheon Airport:** taxis running under the Seoul to Incheon Airport arrangement do not add the out-of-city surcharge, but **tolls** are paid on top of the meter.
- **Tolls** on expressways and bridges are always added to the fare.

## How to hail a taxi on the street

1. **Look for the windshield sign.** A red **빈차** (*bincha*, vacant) light means the taxi is free. A green **예약** (*yeyak*, reserved) sign means it is heading to a booked pickup and will not stop.
2. **Wave your hand** low and diagonally when it approaches. Taxi stands outside stations, hotels and department stores are the easiest spots.
3. **Use the rear doors.** Doors are manual; open and close them yourself.
4. **Show your destination in Korean.** Drivers know Korean place names and addresses far better than English ones. Copy the Korean name or road-name address from Naver Map or Kakao Map and show it on your screen (see our [map apps guide](../google-maps-korea-naver-kakao-map/)).
5. **Fasten your seatbelt.** Rear seatbelts are required on all roads in Korea.
6. **Pay at the end** and take the receipt.

Licensed taxis have yellow number plates. Avoid drivers who approach you inside the airport arrivals hall offering rides; use the official taxi stand outside instead.

## Paying for a taxi

- **Cards:** Most taxis take Visa and Mastercard, including foreign-issued cards. Readers occasionally fail with foreign cards, so carry a backup.
- **T-money or WOWPASS:** You can tap a transport card at the reader, the same card you use on the subway. Our [payment guide](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/) compares the options.
- **Cash:** Accepted, but drivers may not have change for a 50,000 won note, so keep 1,000 and 5,000 won bills.
- **App rides:** If you booked with automatic payment in an app, you are charged in the app. Do not pay the driver again.

Since December 2025, Seoul taxi receipts include English fields for the final fare, boarding and arrival times, and show whether late-night or out-of-city surcharges were applied.

## Taxi apps: Kakao T vs k.ride vs Uber

Booking by app means no language barrier with the driver, a clear pickup point and, in most cases, automatic payment.

| | k.ride | Uber | Kakao T |
|---|---|---|---|
| Made for | Foreign visitors | Visitors already using Uber | Locals and residents |
| Run by | Kakao Mobility | Uber (books licensed taxis) | Kakao Mobility |
| Sign-up | Google, Apple or email; no Kakao account | Your existing Uber account | Kakao account plus phone verification |
| App language | English, Japanese, simplified and traditional Chinese | Your Uber language | Korean, English, Japanese |
| Payment | Foreign cards and some overseas wallets (such as Alipay+ and GrabPay) | Cards and wallets in your Uber account | Foreign Visa, Mastercard, JCB, Amex for taxis; or pay the driver directly |
| Extra cost | 10% service fee on the fare | Uber's fees shown before booking | Regular call: no platform fee; premium types cost more |
| Driver chat | Auto-translated | In-app chat | Korean |

### k.ride: easiest for most visitors

Kakao Mobility launched k.ride in June 2024 as a taxi app just for foreign travelers. It draws on the same taxi network as Kakao T, the largest in Korea, so availability is good in Seoul and other big cities. You sign up with Google, Apple or email, pay with a card from home, and destination search and driver chat are translated across more than 100 languages. The trade-off is a 10% service fee on top of the fare, according to k.ride's published fee schedule as of October 2026.

### Uber: fine if you already have it

Uber works in Korea mainly as **Uber Taxi**, which sends a licensed taxi rather than a private car. You can request it with the account and card you already use at home, and the app is most reliable in Seoul, Busan and Incheon. Coverage outside big cities is thinner, and waits can be longer than on Kakao's network at busy times.

### Kakao T: the local favorite, with a sign-up catch

Kakao T is what most Koreans use, and it has the most cars. Its English mode is under **Settings, Language**. According to Kakao's help pages, you can register a **Visa, Mastercard, JCB or Amex card issued outside Korea** for taxi rides (not for premium Black or Venti rides), and the name on the card should match your account. The catch is sign-up: Kakao T requires phone verification, and many foreign users report that it fails with a non-Korean number. If it works for you, a regular Kakao T call has no extra platform fee, which makes it the cheapest app option. Try registering before your trip so you are not stuck at night.

Kakao T also offers optional types such as **Blue** (regular taxi with a variable call fee), **Venti** (large van, surge pricing) and **Black** (premium). Late at night, Seoul's regular call can be hard to get between about 10 p.m. and 3 a.m.; Kakao T's paid booster call (up to 4,000 won extra) helps then.

## Airport taxis and the International Taxi

From Incheon Airport, a regular metered taxi to central Seoul usually costs roughly 60,000 to 95,000 won plus tolls, depending on traffic and where you are going (an estimate from recent travel guides, not an official fare). Seoul's **International Taxi** service has drivers who speak a foreign language and flat zone fares from the airport, tolls included:

| Seoul zone (example districts) | Mid-size | Large or premium |
|---|---|---|
| A: Gangseo | 70,000 won | 100,000 won |
| B: Mapo, Yeongdeungpo, Seodaemun | 75,000 won | 110,000 won |
| C: Jung (Myeongdong), Jongno, Yongsan | 85,000 won | 120,000 won |
| D: Gangnam, Seocho, Seongdong | 90,000 won | 130,000 won |
| E: Songpa, Gangdong, Gwangjin | 95,000 won | 140,000 won |

Book in advance or ask at the International Taxi desk in the arrivals area. If you are traveling light, the AREX train is much cheaper; see our [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](../incheon-airport-to-seoul/).

## Tips for a smooth ride

- **No tipping.** Regular taxis do not expect a tip. Kakao T has an optional in-app "thank-you tip" for some premium types only.
- **Note the plate number.** It is on the receipt and in the app, and you will need it to report a problem or find a lost item.
- **Problems or overcharging:** call the **Seoul 120 Dasan Call Center** or scan the complaint QR code inside the taxi. The **1330** Korea Travel Hotline offers 24-hour multilingual help and takes tourist complaints. Keep your receipt and app booking details.
- **Late on weekends,** expect a wait around nightlife areas like Hongdae, Itaewon and Gangnam. Walking a block away from the crowd, or booking through an app, usually helps.
- **Long trips:** for Seoul to Busan, the KTX is faster and far cheaper than a taxi; see our [KTX guide](../seoul-to-busan-ktx/).

## FAQ

**Can I use a foreign credit card in Korean taxis?**
Usually yes. Most taxis take foreign Visa and Mastercard at the card reader, and k.ride, Uber and Kakao T all accept foreign cards for app payment. Carry T-money or some cash as a backup in case a reader fails.

**Is Uber available in Korea?**
Yes, mainly as Uber Taxi, which books a licensed taxi through the Uber app. It works best in Seoul, Busan and Incheon.

**Do I need a Korean phone number for Kakao T?**
Kakao T requires phone verification at sign-up, and it often fails with foreign numbers. If you cannot register, use k.ride, which signs you up with Google, Apple or email.

**How much is a taxi in Seoul at night?**
As of November 2026, a regular taxi's base fare is 5,800 won from 10 to 11 p.m. and 2 to 4 a.m., and 6,700 won from 11 p.m. to 2 a.m., compared with 4,800 won by day. Deluxe and large taxis cost 20% more from 10 p.m. to 4 a.m.

**Should I tip taxi drivers in Korea?**
No. Tipping is not expected in Korean taxis. Paying the metered fare is enough.

**What is the difference between orange and black taxis?**
Orange, white and silver taxis are regular taxis with the cheapest fares. Black taxis are usually deluxe (mobeom) taxis with a 7,000 won base fare, more legroom and often more experienced drivers.
