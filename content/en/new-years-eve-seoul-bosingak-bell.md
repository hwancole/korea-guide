The biggest New Year's Eve tradition in Seoul is the **Jeya-ui Jong (제야의 종)**, the midnight ringing of the bell at **Bosingak Pavilion** in Jongno. Tens of thousands of people pack the streets to hear it, and it is broadcast live across Korea, a bit like the Times Square ball drop or Japan's *joya no kane*.

> **Quick answer:** The Bosingak bell is struck **33 times at midnight** on 31 December. Watching is **free** and needs no ticket or registration. Last year's programme ran from about **10:50 PM to 12:20 AM**, with performances before and after the bell. **Jonggak Station (Line 1) does not stop** from 11 PM to 1 AM, so use City Hall, Jongno 3-ga, Euljiro 1-ga or Gwanghwamun stations instead. Subway and many buses run until about **2 AM**. Arrive by **10 PM** if you want to see the pavilion itself, and dress for temperatures well below zero.

As of November 2026, Seoul has not yet published the plan for 31 December 2026 (it usually does so in the last week of December). The details below come from the official plan for the last event, on 31 December 2025, and the event has followed the same format for years. Check the update links at the end before you go.

## Why 33 strikes?

The number comes from the Joseon-era custom of ringing the bell 33 times at dawn (*parujeong*) to open the city gates of Hanyang, old Seoul. It is linked to the 33 heavens of Buddhist cosmology and is meant to wish peace and good fortune on the whole country. The midnight New Year version has been held at Bosingak since 1953. The bell you will hear is a replica: the original 15th-century bell was retired for preservation and is now kept at the National Museum of Korea.

## What happens on the night

Based on the last event, the evening runs like this:

1. **About 10:50–11:50 PM:** stage performances in front of Bosingak, such as traditional music, pansori and a choir.
2. **About 11:55 PM:** the countdown is projected onto the pavilion roof and nearby screens, and the crowd counts down together.
3. **Midnight:** the bell is struck 33 times, which takes several minutes. A media façade lights up the building beside the pavilion.
4. **After midnight to about 12:20 AM:** a short concert by a popular band (Crying Nut in 2025), then the crowd disperses.

You will not see much of the bell itself unless you are near the front. Most people watch on the large screens and listen to the sound carry down Jongno.

## Where to stand

- **Directly in front of Bosingak (Jonggak intersection):** the best view but the densest crowd. People start claiming spots by 9–10 PM.
- **Along Jongno toward Gwanghwamun:** easier to move, with screens and the sound of the bell, and a quick walk to Gwanghwamun Square if you want a second countdown.
- **Gwanghwamun Square:** Seoul held a separate countdown show here last year (about 10:50 PM to 12:30 AM), with media art projected onto Gwanghwamun Gate and eight nearby buildings plus live music. It is a good option if you want space and visuals more than the bell.

Police set up one-way walking routes and crowd-control lines around the intersection. Follow staff directions, keep moving at choke points, and agree a meeting spot with your group in advance, because mobile networks can slow down at midnight. Last year, messaging apps briefly struggled right after 12 AM.

## Getting there and back

### Subway

| Station | Lines | Walk to Bosingak |
|---|---|---|
| Jonggak | 1 | Next door, but **no stops 23:00–01:00** |
| Jongno 3-ga | 1, 3, 5 | About 10 minutes |
| City Hall | 1, 2 | About 10–15 minutes |
| Euljiro 1-ga | 2 | About 10 minutes |
| Gwanghwamun | 5 | About 10–15 minutes |

Last year, Seoul Metro Lines 1–9 plus the Ui-Sinseol and Sillim lines ran **104 extra trains**, timed so the last trains arrived at their terminals by about 2 AM. That does not mean a train leaves central Seoul at 2 AM. For example, the last trains through City Hall and Jongno 3-ga passed around 1:40–1:55 AM toward points only a few stops away. If your hotel is far out, or you need a Gyeonggi or Incheon line, look up the last train from your exact station in Naver Map or KakaoMap and plan to leave the area by about 12:45 AM. Our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/) explains how to use the apps and T-money.

### Buses and taxis

From 6 PM on 31 December to 7 AM on 1 January, around 47 city bus routes are detoured away from Jongno, so buses do not stop at Bosingak. Last year, 45 routes that serve the area extended their last runs to about 2 AM. Taxis are very hard to find right after midnight near the closures. Walk several blocks away first, for example toward Euljiro or Anguk, before calling one on Kakao T (see our [Kakao T taxi guide](../taxi-in-korea-kakao-t-guide/)).

### Bikes and e-scooters

Public Ttareungyi bike stations near Jongno are temporarily closed during the event, and shared e-scooters cannot be rented or returned around Bosingak from 6 PM to 6 AM.

## What to wear and bring

Seoul on New Year's Eve is usually well below freezing at midnight, and you will stand still for an hour or more. Wear a long padded coat, thermal layers, gloves, a hat and warm boots, and bring hand warmers (sold at any convenience store). Our [Korean winter weather guide](../korea-winter-weather-what-to-wear/) has a full packing list. Seoul set up heated cold-weather shelters and a first-aid booth near the Jonggak intersection last year. Charge your phone fully, carry a T-money or WOWPASS card for the ride back, and use a toilet before 10 PM: queues get long and public toilets around the square are limited.

## Other ways to welcome the New Year in Seoul

| Option | What it is (last event) | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Gwanghwamun Square countdown | Media art on Gwanghwamun Gate and nearby buildings, live music, ~22:50–00:30 | Big visuals, a bit more space |
| DDP (Dongdaemun) | Countdown and light show on the curved façade of Dongdaemun Design Plaza | Combining with late-night shopping |
| Lotte World Tower (Jamsil) | Countdown with a laser light show on Korea's tallest building | Watching from Seokchon Lake, away from Jongno |
| Myeongdong | Countdown on the giant LED screen of Shinsegae Department Store | Easy if you stay in Myeongdong |
| First sunrise on 1 January | Namsan, Achasan and other hills; sunrise is around 7:45 AM | Korea's favourite New Year ritual |

Korea's most popular New Year activity is actually watching the first sunrise (*haedoji*) on 1 January. In Seoul, Achasan in the east is the classic hike, but trail entrances closed once 6,000 hikers had entered last year, so go early. Namsan is easier, and Seoul runs a sunrise shuttle schedule that changes each year.

## Is anything closed on 1 January?

New Year's Day (Sinjeong, 신정) is a public holiday, but it is a quiet one. Banks and government offices close, and some restaurants take the day off. Most shops, department stores, cafés and attractions stay open, and palaces are generally open as usual unless 1 January falls on their regular closing day. Korea's big family holiday is the Lunar New Year (Seollal), which falls in early February in 2027 and is when far more businesses close.

## Planning checklist

1. Around 26–30 December, check the Seoul city announcement for the year's performers, closures and last trains.
2. Pick a nearby station other than Jonggak, and save your hotel's last train time.
3. Have dinner before 9 PM. Restaurants near Jongno fill up and some close early.
4. Arrive by 10 PM for a spot near the pavilion, or 11:30 PM if you are happy watching the screens.
5. After the bell, walk away from the intersection before heading to the subway, since the nearest entrances get crowded.

## FAQ

**Do I need a ticket for the Bosingak bell ringing?**
No. Watching is free and there is no registration. The only people with assigned places are the official bell ringers and invited guests.

**Can tourists ring the Bosingak bell?**
Not at midnight on New Year's Eve, when the ringers are pre-selected. Bosingak also holds regular daytime bell-ringing ceremonies with a guard ceremony on some days, but those are run on their own schedule, so check before you go.

**Are there fireworks in Seoul on New Year's Eve?**
There is no large city fireworks show at Bosingak. The main events use projection mapping, LED screens and laser shows instead. Last year, Lotte World Tower used a laser and lighting show for its countdown.

**What time should I leave to catch the last subway?**
Leave the Bosingak area by about 12:45 AM if you need to go far. Last trains passed central stations around 1:40–1:55 AM last year, but crowded platforms mean you may not get on the first train that arrives.

**Is it safe?**
The event is heavily policed, with thousands of police and safety staff, one-way routes and crowd monitoring. Keep to the marked routes, avoid narrow side alleys at peak times, and keep valuables in an inside pocket.

**Is 31 December a holiday in Korea?**
No. New Year's Eve is a normal working day, and 1 January is the public holiday. In 2026, 31 December is a Thursday and 1 January 2027 is a Friday.
