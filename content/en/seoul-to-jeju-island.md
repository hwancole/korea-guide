Jeju is an island about 450 km south of Seoul, so there is no train or bridge. You either **fly** or combine a **train or bus with a ferry**. For almost every visitor, flying is the obvious choice: it is fast, frequent and often cheaper than the land-and-sea route.

> **Quick answer:** Fly from **Gimpo Airport (GMP)** in western Seoul to **Jeju International Airport (CJU)**. The flight is about **1 hour 5 to 1 hour 15 minutes**, and Gimpo–Jeju is the busiest air route in the world, with about **39,000 seats a day** in 2025 (OAG). Off-peak one-way fares on budget airlines are often in the ₩25,000 to ₩70,000 range, but weekends and holidays cost much more. Foreigners board with their **passport**. The slower alternative is a **KTX train to Mokpo plus a 4.5-hour ferry**, which makes sense mainly if you are bringing a car or want the sea journey.

Fares, timetables and bus routes below are as of November 2026. Airline prices change daily, so treat any number here as a guide, not a quote.

## Your options at a glance

| Route | Total time (door to door, approx.) | Typical cost one way (as of Nov 2026) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight Gimpo → Jeju | 3 to 3.5 hours including getting to the airport | About ₩25,000 to ₩70,000 off-peak; ₩100,000+ on peak dates | Almost everyone |

## Flying from Gimpo (the standard way)

**Gimpo Airport** handles almost all domestic flights from Seoul. **Incheon**, the big international airport, has only a handful of Jeju flights, so do not assume you can fly to Jeju from there unless you have a connecting ticket.

### Getting to Gimpo Airport

Gimpo is on the subway network, which makes it easy:

- **AREX (Airport Railroad)** from **Seoul Station** or **Hongik University (Hongdae)**: the all-stop train reaches Gimpo in roughly 15 to 25 minutes.
- **Line 5** (purple) and **Line 9** (gold) both stop at Gimpo International Airport Station. Line 9 express trains are handy from Gangnam and Yeouido.
- Follow the signs for the **Domestic Terminal**, which is separate from the international one.

Pay with a T-money card or a credit card that supports transit, just like any other subway trip. See our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/) if you have not used it yet.

### Which airlines fly Gimpo–Jeju

Nine Korean airlines flew the route in 2026, according to Asia Business Daily. They include full-service **Korean Air** and **Asiana**, plus low-cost carriers such as **Jeju Air**, **Jin Air**, **T'way Air** and **Eastar Jet**. Jeju Air alone runs up to 26 round trips a day. In practice there are departures roughly every few minutes from early morning to late evening.

Low-cost carriers usually include a smaller free checked bag allowance than Korean Air and Asiana, so compare the price **with your luggage** before you book.

### How to book

1. **Compare first.** Search on Google Flights, Skyscanner or Trip.com to see which airlines are cheapest for your date.
2. **Book on the airline's own website or app** if you can. Most Korean airlines have English sites and accept foreign credit cards. Booking direct makes changes and refunds easier.
3. **Book early for peak dates.** Weekends, school holidays (late July to August), Chuseok, Lunar New Year (Seollal) and the spring cherry blossom season sell out and fares can jump several times over.
4. **Use the passport name.** Enter your name exactly as it appears in your passport.

### ID and airport rules for foreigners

- **Bring your passport.** You must show an original photo ID to board domestic flights in Korea. For foreigners, that means your **passport**. Korean Air also lists an alien registration card and a driver's license among accepted IDs, but a passport is the safe choice. A photo of it on your phone is not accepted.
- **Check-in closes 30 minutes before departure**, and **boarding closes 10 minutes before departure** on Korean Air (as of November 2026). Other airlines have similar rules. Arrive at Gimpo about **1 hour before** a domestic flight, more on weekends and holidays.
- **Security is quicker than international**, but battery rules are the same: power banks and spare lithium batteries must go in your **carry-on**, never in checked luggage.
- Domestic flights do not go through immigration, so there is no passport stamp and no arrival card.

## The train and ferry route via Mokpo

If you want to travel by sea, the most practical option from Seoul is to take a **KTX** from **Yongsan Station** (or **Suseo Station** in southeast Seoul) to **Mokpo** on the southwest coast, then a ferry across to Jeju.

- **Train:** KTX trains from Yongsan reach Mokpo in roughly 2.5 hours. Since the September 2026 KTX–SRT merger, both stations are bookable in the **Korail+** app. Check the app for the current fare.
- **Ferry:** Seaworld Express Ferry runs the Mokpo–Jeju crossing, typically **one to two sailings a day**, taking **about 4.5 hours** (as of November 2026). Economy floor-seat tickets start around ₩32,000, with reclining seats and private cabins costing more.
- **Getting from Mokpo Station to the ferry terminal:** take a taxi; it is a short ride.

Other ferry ports to Jeju include **Wando** (shorter crossing, but Wando is a long bus ride from Seoul) and **Yeosu**. Schedules change with the season and the weather, and sailings are cancelled in high wind or rough seas, which is more common in winter. Book on the ferry company's site or an aggregator such as Direct Ferries, and check the day before.

**Who should take the ferry?** Mainly people bringing their own car or motorbike, and travelers who want the journey to be part of the trip. For everyone else, the flight saves most of a day.

## Getting around after you land at Jeju Airport

Jeju Airport is on the north coast in **Jeju City**. Where you go next depends on where you are staying.

| Destination | Option (as of Nov 2026) | Approx. fare | Approx. time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jeju City hotels | Regular city bus or taxi | Bus about ₩1,200; taxi about ₩9,000 to City Hall | 10 to 30 min |
| Jungmun resort area | Airport limousine bus **600** | ₩4,500 | About 50 to 60 min |
| Seogwipo city / hotels | Limousine bus **600** or **800** | ₩5,000 to ₩5,500 | About 1 to 1.5 hours |
| Seongsan (Sunrise Peak) | Express bus or taxi | Taxi around ₩45,000 | 1 to 1.5 hours |
| Anywhere on the island | Rental car | From airport desks | — |

A few practical points:

- **Buses take transport cards.** Jeju buses accept nationally compatible transit cards and many credit cards with transit features; card users get a small discount and free transfers within a short window. If you already have a **T-money** or WOWPASS card from Seoul, bring it. Our [guide to paying in Korea](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/) explains the cards.
- **Limousine bus 600** leaves from in front of the arrivals hall (Gate 5 area) roughly every 20 minutes from about 06:00 until late evening, stopping at the big Jungmun resort hotels before Seogwipo.
- **Taxis** are plentiful at the airport. Kakao T works on Jeju too; see our [taxi guide](../taxi-in-korea-kakao-t-guide/).
- **Renting a car** is the most flexible way to see Jeju, because many sights are spread out along the coast. Foreign visitors need their **home driver's license plus an International Driving Permit (IDP)** from a country that recognizes the Geneva or Vienna conventions. Without one, the rental desk will not give you a car.

## Tips for planning the trip

- **Fly early on day one and late on the last day.** With flights about every few minutes, you can easily make it a two- or three-day trip without losing much time.
- **Check the weather.** Jeju is windier and wetter than Seoul. Winter is milder (often above freezing), but strong wind can delay flights and cancel ferries. See [Korea winter weather and what to wear](../korea-winter-weather-what-to-wear/).
- **Do not count on Incheon.** If you land at Incheon from abroad and want to go straight to Jeju, look for a through ticket from your airline, or transfer to Gimpo (about an hour by AREX or bus).

## FAQ

**How long is the flight from Seoul to Jeju?**
About 1 hour 5 to 1 hour 15 minutes from Gimpo, depending on the airline and wind.

**Which Seoul airport should I fly from?**
Gimpo (GMP). It has almost all of the Jeju flights. Incheon has very few.

**Do foreigners need a passport for a domestic flight in Korea?**
Yes. You need an original photo ID, and for most visitors that is the passport. Keep it with you, not in your checked bag.

**How much does a Seoul–Jeju flight cost?**
Off-peak one-way fares on low-cost airlines are often around ₩25,000 to ₩70,000 as of November 2026. Weekends, holidays and summer can cost ₩100,000 or more. Remember to add baggage fees on budget airlines.

**Can I take a train to Jeju?**
Not directly. Jeju is an island. You can take a KTX to Mokpo and then a ferry of about 4.5 hours.

**Is the ferry cheaper than flying?**
Usually not. Once you add the train or bus to the port, the total is often higher than an off-peak flight, and the trip takes most of a day.

**Can I use my T-money card on Jeju?**
Jeju buses accept nationally compatible transit cards, which includes the cards most visitors buy in Seoul. Card users also get a small discount.

**Do I need a car on Jeju?**
Not strictly. Buses reach most major sights, but they can be slow and infrequent outside Jeju City and Seogwipo. A rental car (with an International Driving Permit) or taxis save a lot of time.

**What if my flight or ferry is cancelled because of weather?**
Airlines rebook you on a later flight. Ferries are cancelled more often in strong wind. Call the Korea Travel Helpline at 1330 (+82-2-1330 from a foreign phone) for help in English.
