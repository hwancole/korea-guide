The fastest and simplest way from Seoul to Busan is the **KTX high-speed train**. Trains leave all day from central Seoul, the ride takes about two and a half hours, and you arrive at Busan Station in the middle of the city, connected to the subway. Since **1 September 2026** the former SRT trains are part of KTX too, so one app and one fare table now cover departures from both Seoul Station and Suseo Station.

> **Quick answer:** A standard KTX seat from **Seoul Station to Busan Station costs ₩54,400 one way** (as of October 2026, down from ₩59,800 before the merger) and takes **about 2 hours 30 minutes**. Leave from **Seoul Station** if you are staying north of the Han River (Myeongdong, Hongdae, Jongno) and from **Suseo Station** if you are staying in Gangnam or southeast Seoul. Book in the **Korail+ app** or on Korail's English website; tickets go on sale **two months ahead at 7:00 a.m.** and weekend trains sell out.

Fares, rules and app features below are as of October 2026. The merged rail operator is still rolling out changes, so check the booking screen for the final price before you pay.

## What changed in September 2026

Until August 2026, South Korea had two high-speed operators on the Seoul–Busan line: Korail ran **KTX** from Seoul and Yongsan stations, and a separate company, SR, ran **SRT** from Suseo Station in southeast Seoul. They had different apps, different booking accounts and slightly different fares, which confused many visitors.

From 1 September 2026 the two were merged under Korail:

- **One brand.** The SRT name is being retired. Its trains now run as **KTX-Sancheon** and will be repainted over the coming years, so you may still see purple SRT livery on the platform.
- **Lower fares.** KTX fares were cut by about 10% on average to match the old SRT prices. Seoul–Busan standard class fell from ₩59,800 to ₩54,400.
- **One app.** The **Korail+** app searches and books trains from Seoul, Yongsan and Suseo stations together. The old SRT app is being phased out.
- **More seats.** Korail added roughly 15,000 high-speed seats a day, with the biggest increase on trains from Suseo.

## Your options from Seoul to Busan

| Option | One-way fare (as of Oct 2026) | Journey time | From → to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KTX** (standard seat) | ₩54,400 | About 2 h 30 min | Seoul Station → Busan Station |
| **KTX-Sancheon from Suseo** (former SRT) | Same fare level as KTX | About 2 h 30 min | Suseo Station → Busan Station |
| **Mugunghwa** (conventional train) | About ₩28,600 | About 5–6 h | Seoul Station → Busan Station |
| **Express bus** (standard / premium) | About ₩26,700 / ₩43,900–48,000 | About 4–4 h 30 min, more in traffic | Seoul Express Bus Terminal → Busan |
| **Domestic flight** | About ₩40,000–80,000, varies by date | About 1 h in the air, plus airport time | Gimpo → Gimhae |

KTX first class costs more than standard; the merger cut first-class fares by a different amount, so check the exact figure on the booking screen. Bus and flight prices are approximate and change by operator and date.

For most travelers the KTX wins: it is city-center to city-center, it is not affected by highway traffic, and you do not need to arrive early for security checks the way you do at an airport.

## Seoul Station or Suseo Station?

Both stations now sell the same KTX product to Busan, so choose the one that is easier to reach from your hotel.

| | Seoul Station | Suseo Station |
|---|---|---|
| Area | Central Seoul, north of the Han River | Gangnam-gu, southeast Seoul |
| Subway | Lines 1 and 4, Airport Railroad (AREX), Gyeongui-Jungang Line | Line 3, Suin-Bundang Line, GTX-A |
| Best if you stay in | Myeongdong, Jongno, Insadong, Hongdae, Itaewon | Gangnam, Jamsil, COEX, Sinsa |

If you are coming straight from Incheon Airport, AREX runs directly to Seoul Station, which makes Seoul Station the easier choice. See our [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](../incheon-airport-to-seoul/) for that leg.

## How to book a KTX ticket

### Option 1: Korail+ app

1. Download **Korail+** (the official app, replacing Korail Talk). It offers English, Japanese, Chinese and other languages, though a few pop-ups may still appear in Korean.
2. Search **Seoul** (or **Suseo**) to **Busan** and your date. Both departure stations can appear in the same results.
3. Choose a train, then pick standard or first class and a seat.
4. Pay by card. Korail has accepted foreign-issued credit cards for foreign users, but some travelers still report payment failures, so keep the website or a station counter as a backup.
5. Your ticket is a QR code in the app. There are no ticket gates at Korean train stations; just board and sit in your assigned seat. Staff check tickets on board.

### Option 2: Korail English website

Korail's English website lets foreign visitors search, book and print or save e-tickets. It is a good choice if the app will not accept your phone number or card.

### Option 3: Station ticket counter or machine

You can buy tickets at the Travel Center counter or ticket machines at Seoul, Suseo and Busan stations, and pay by card or cash. This works well on weekdays, but on Fridays, Sundays and holidays the trains you want may already be full.

### When tickets go on sale

From October 2026, regular tickets open **two months before departure at 7:00 a.m.** Korea time (previously one month). For example, a train on 31 December goes on sale on 31 October at 7:00 a.m. National holidays such as Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok have separate sale dates and sell out within minutes.

If your train is full, you can join a waiting list up to two days before departure, or buy a **standing ticket** (about 15% cheaper, no seat guaranteed). Some weekday trains also have an unreserved car, sold about 5% cheaper.

## Is the Korail Pass worth it for Busan?

The **Korail Pass** is sold only to foreign visitors with a passport and gives unlimited rides on Korail trains (KTX, ITX-Saemaeul, Mugunghwa and others) on 2 to 5 travel days chosen within a 10-day period. Prices as of October 2026:

| Pass | Adult (13+) | Child (6–12) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 days | ₩131,000 | ₩66,000 |
| 3 days | ₩186,000 | ₩93,000 |
| 4 days | ₩234,000 | ₩117,000 |
| 5 days | ₩275,000 | ₩138,000 |

A simple Seoul–Busan round trip at the new fare costs ₩108,800, which is **less than a 2-day pass**. The pass only pays off if you add more legs, for example Seoul → Gyeongju → Busan → Seoul, or Seoul → Busan plus a trip to Gangneung, within the same days.

Things to know before buying:

- Reserve seats on the Korail website or at a Travel Center; you get up to two seat reservations per day.
- Pass sellers have not clearly stated whether the pass now covers the former SRT trains from Suseo. Until that is confirmed, plan to use Seoul or Yongsan departures with the pass.
- Seats may not be available to pass holders during major holidays, when you may have to stand.

## Changes and refunds

Since the merger, all high-speed tickets follow the same rules (as of October 2026):

- **Changing a ticket:** In Korail+ you can switch to another train on the same route free of charge up to 30 minutes before departure, for a train within 7 days of the original date. You pay any fare difference.
- **Refunds within 10 minutes of payment** are free.

Refund fees for standard tickets:

| When you cancel | Mon–Thu trains | Fri–Sun and holiday trains |
|---|---|---|
| 2+ days before departure | Free | ₩400 |
| 1 day before | Free | 5% |
| Departure day, until 3 h before | Free | 10% |
| 3 h before until departure | 5% | 20% |
| Up to 20 min after departure | 15% | 30% |
| 20–60 min after departure | 40% | 40% |
| 60 min after departure until arrival | 70% | 70% |

**Delay compensation** was also improved: a delay of 90 minutes or more refunds 75% of the fare, and 120 minutes or more refunds the full fare.

## On the train and arriving in Busan

- **Luggage:** There is no checked baggage. Use the overhead racks for small bags and the luggage space at the end of each car for suitcases. Large suitcases fit, but space fills up on busy trains.
- **Power:** Many seats have power outlets, but not all, so check the seat map when booking if you need to charge a phone.
- **Food:** You can eat on board. Buy snacks or a lunch box (dosirak) at the station before boarding, as on-board sales are limited.
- **Arriving:** Busan Station is on **Busan Metro Line 1**. For Haeundae, take Line 1 to Seomyeon and change to Line 2; allow about an hour door to door. Nampo-dong and Jagalchi Market are only a few stops south on Line 1.

## FAQ

**How long is the KTX from Seoul to Busan?**
About 2 hours 30 minutes on most trains. Trains with more stops take a little longer, so check the arrival time on your ticket.

**How much is the KTX from Seoul to Busan in 2026?**
₩54,400 one way in standard class as of October 2026, after the September 2026 fare cut. First class costs more. Discounts for young travelers and online specials can lower the price further for some trains.

**Is SRT still running?**
The trains still run, but since 1 September 2026 they are part of KTX and operate as KTX-Sancheon from Suseo Station. Book them in Korail+ like any other KTX train.

**Can I buy a ticket on the day?**
Usually yes on weekday mornings and middays. On Friday evenings, weekends and holidays, book days or weeks ahead.

**Do I need to print my ticket?**
No. A QR code in the app or a saved e-ticket is enough. There are no gates; a conductor checks tickets on board.

**Is a day trip from Seoul to Busan possible?**
Yes. With about five hours of round-trip travel, you can leave early, spend six to eight hours in Busan and be back in Seoul at night. An overnight stay is more relaxing if you want to see both Haeundae and the old town.

**Is the bus cheaper than the KTX?**
Yes, a standard express bus costs about half the KTX fare, but the journey is around four hours or more and depends on traffic.
