Prices in Korean shops include 10% VAT, and short-term visitors can get most of that tax back on goods they take home. The system is generous: the minimum spend is low, and at many stores you never have to queue at the airport at all, because the tax comes off at the till.

> **Quick answer:** If you are a foreign visitor staying **less than six months**, spend **₩15,000 or more on one receipt** at a store with a **Tax Free / Tax Refund** sign and show your **physical passport**. At stores offering an **immediate refund**, you simply pay the lower price (each purchase must be under ₩1,000,000, up to ₩5,000,000 per trip). For other purchases, keep the refund receipt and claim at a **downtown refund booth** or at the **airport refund kiosk** before you fly. Take the goods out of Korea **within three months**. Rules as of November 2026.

## Who can claim a tax refund in Korea?

According to the Korea Tourism Organization (VISITKOREA), you qualify if you are:

- a **non-Korean citizen** staying in Korea for **less than six months**, or
- an **overseas Korean** staying less than three months who has lived abroad for more than two years or holds permanent residency in another country.

You do not qualify if you work in Korea, have stayed six months or longer, or are buying for a business. Goods must be new and unused, and you have to leave Korea with them **within three months of the purchase date**. The passport has to be shown at the moment you pay; most stores cannot add it to the receipt later, so carry the real passport (not a photo) on shopping days.

## The three ways to get your money back

| Method | Purchase per receipt | Trip limit | When you get the money |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Immediate refund** at the store | ₩15,000 to under ₩1,000,000 | ₩5,000,000 total | At the till: you pay the price minus the refund |
| **Downtown refund booth** | ₩15,000 to ₩6,000,000 | No set limit | In the city, before you leave, in cash or to a card |
| **Airport or port refund** | ₩15,000 or more | No purchase limit | At departure, at a kiosk or counter |

*Limits as of November 2026, from VISITKOREA's tax refund guide. The immediate refund limits were doubled and the minimum was lowered from ₩30,000 to ₩15,000 on January 1, 2024.*

### 1. Immediate refund (the easiest)

Look for a sign saying **Immediate Tax Refund** or **Tax Free** with a note about instant refunds. This is common at K-beauty chains, department stores, duty-free-style shops and big shopping streets like Myeongdong and Hongdae. Tell the cashier "tax free" before paying and hand over your passport. The VAT refund is deducted on the spot.

Points to know:

- Each payment must be **at least ₩15,000 and under ₩1,000,000**.
- Your total immediate refunds for the trip can't exceed **₩5,000,000** in purchases. Once you pass that, later stores will issue a normal refund receipt instead.
- The store links the purchase to your passport electronically. You normally do not need to show these goods at the airport, but you still have to take them out of Korea within three months. If you don't, the tax can be charged back to your card at departure.

### 2. Downtown refund booths

If the store gives you a refund receipt instead (often for bigger purchases), you can cash it in at a city refund booth before your trip ends, for example in Myeongdong or inside department stores. Bring the **goods, the refund receipt, your passport and an international credit card**. Each receipt can be up to ₩6,000,000.

Two catches, as of November 2026:

- Each booth only pays out for **its own refund company's** receipts (Global Tax Free, Global Blue, Easy Tax Refund and others). Airport counters accept all of them.
- A downtown refund is a kind of advance. You still have to confirm at the airport refund kiosk or customs desk when you leave; the card you give the booth acts as a guarantee if you don't.

### 3. At the airport (Incheon, Gimpo, Gimhae, Jeju)

This is the catch-all for every refund receipt you haven't already used. At Incheon Airport the steps are:

1. **Check in, but hold back the shopping bags.** If refund items are in a suitcase you are checking, tell the airline staff. They will tag the bag and send you to the customs desk, where the bag is inspected and then put on the large conveyor next to the desk.
2. **Use a tax refund kiosk.** Kiosks are in the departure hall near the customs desks. Scan your passport (and receipts if asked). The kiosk tells you whether customs needs to see the goods.
3. **Customs check if required.** If the kiosk flags your purchase, show the unused items, receipts and passport to a customs officer. Refund operators say this mainly applies to large refunds, roughly **₩75,000 or more in refund per receipt**, but follow what the kiosk says.
4. **Collect the money after security.** Cash is paid at the refund counters and kiosks in the duty-free area.

| Incheon Airport location | Terminal 1 | Terminal 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Refund counter (duty-free area) | 3rd floor near Gate 28, 07:00–22:00 | 3rd floor near Gate 253 (across from Gate 250), 07:00–21:30 |
| Refund kiosks | Departure hall near customs desks, and 4th floor near Gates 26–27 | Departure hall near customs desks, and near Gate 253 |

*Locations and hours from the Incheon Airport website, as of November 2026.* If the counter is closed or you are short on time, you can drop the completed receipt in the refund box. The money then goes to your credit card, Alipay or WeChat Pay, which usually takes one to two months.

Allow an extra **30–60 minutes** at the airport if you have refunds to process, especially on evening flights from Incheon when lines get long.

## How much do you actually get back?

Not quite 10%. The tax is 10% on top of the base price, so it is about 9.1% of the price you pay, and the refund companies keep a fee. They pay a fixed amount for each price band. As an example, a Global Tax Free table published in January 2026 gave:

| Purchase on one receipt | Refund |
|---|---|
| ₩50,000–74,999 | ₩3,000 |
| ₩100,000–124,999 | ₩7,000 |
| ₩200,000–224,999 | ₩12,000 |

So expect roughly **5–7%** back on mid-sized purchases with a refund receipt. Immediate refunds at the till usually give a similar amount. The exact figure is printed on your receipt.

## Tips to get the most from it

- **Combine purchases into one receipt.** The ₩15,000 minimum and the price bands apply per receipt, so one ₩60,000 basket is better than three ₩20,000 ones. Receipts from different stores can't be combined.
- **Pay by card or cash; both work.** The refund doesn't depend on payment method. See our [guide to paying in Korea](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/) for card tips.
- **Keep the goods unopened** until you are through airport checks if you'll claim at the airport. Opened cosmetics can be refused.
- **Don't check refund items at the last minute.** Decide what goes in the hold before you queue, so you don't have to come back.
- **Hotel VAT refund** is a separate scheme: VAT on stays of up to 30 nights at hotels designated by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism can be refunded. Ask the hotel at check-in. The government has proposed ending it early, in June 2027, so check before relying on it.
- **Cosmetic surgery and skin clinic VAT refunds** for foreigners ended on December 31, 2025, and had not been revived as of November 2026.

## FAQ

**Do I need to show my items at the airport?**
Usually not for immediate refunds. For airport claims, only if the kiosk or refund staff tell you to, which mostly happens for large refunds. Keep items accessible just in case.

**Can I claim a refund on food or restaurant bills?**
No. Restaurant meals, services and things you use up in Korea are not covered. Packaged food you take home, such as snacks or ginseng from a store with a Tax Free sign, can qualify.

**Is Olive Young tax free?**
Many Olive Young stores in tourist areas offer immediate tax refunds on purchases of ₩15,000 or more. Ask at the till and show your passport. Policies vary by branch.

**What if I forget to claim before I fly?**
For airport-claim receipts, the chance is mostly gone; the export has to be confirmed when you leave. Some refund companies accept mailed claims with proof of export, but don't count on it.

**I'm leaving by ferry from Busan. Can I still claim?**
Yes. International ports such as Busan have refund counters and customs desks too. Give yourself extra time.

**Do I get the refund in won?**
Cash is usually in won, though some counters pay in US dollars, Chinese yuan or Japanese yen. Card and Alipay/WeChat Pay refunds are also available.
