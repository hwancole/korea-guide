N Seoul Tower sits on top of Namsan, the mountain in the middle of Seoul, and is the city's best-known viewpoint. You will see it called by several names: **Namsan Seoul Tower** (남산서울타워) is the name the city uses, **YTN Seoul Tower** is the owner's brand for the whole site, and **N Seoul Tower** is the name of the observatory and restaurants run inside it. They are all the same place.

> **Quick answer:** The plaza, the love locks and the views from the base are **free**. Going up to the indoor observatory costs **29,000 won for adults** and 23,000 won for children (3 to 12) and seniors (65+), as of November 2026. The observatory is open **10:00 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. on weekdays** and **until 11:00 p.m. on weekends**. The easiest way up is the **Namsan cable car** (15,000 won round trip, 12,000 won one way for adults) from near **Myeongdong Station (Line 4)**, or the **01A/01B Namsan circulation bus** from Chungmuro or Dongguk University Station, which drops you near the top for a normal bus fare.

Prices and hours below are as of November 2026, from Visit Seoul, the tower's official site (seoultower.co.kr) and recent travel listings. The operators change prices from time to time, so check again before you go.

## What you pay for (and what is free)

Many visitors do not realise that most of the experience costs nothing. The mountain park, the walking paths, the plaza at the foot of the tower, the "Locks of Love" fences and the octagonal Palgakjeong pavilion are all open to everyone. You only pay for the indoor observatory high up in the tower, and for the cable car if you take it.

| Ticket | Adult | Child / senior | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| N Seoul Tower observatory | 29,000 won (13+) | 23,000 won (ages 3 to 12, and 65+) | Under 36 months free |
| Namsan cable car, round trip | 15,000 won (13+) | 11,500 won (36 months to 12) | Seniors 65+ pay the child fare with ID |
| Namsan cable car, one way | 12,000 won | 9,000 won | |
| Namsan circulation bus 01A/01B | Standard city bus fare | | Card only, no cash |
| Namsan Oreumi elevator | Free | Free | Myeongdong side, to the cable car station |

All prices as of November 2026. Discounts for visitors with disabilities are available at the observatory counter; bring proof.

**Is the observatory worth it?** On a clear evening, the 360-degree view over the whole city, from the Han River to the mountains in the north, is the main reason people come. On a hazy day, which is common in spring when fine dust and yellow dust blow in, the view from the free plaza is nearly as good, and you can skip the ticket. Check the sky before you buy.

Observatory tickets are sold in the tower itself. Travel apps such as Klook also sell discounted observatory tickets and combo tickets with the cable car; read what the combo includes, since the observatory-only ticket does **not** include the cable car.

The observatory is open every day of the year. Plan to arrive at least 30 minutes before closing, because ticket sales stop before the doors close. The cable car and the inclined elevator can stop in strong wind or heavy rain.

## How to get up Namsan: four ways

### 1. Namsan cable car (the classic way)

The cable car (남산케이블카) has run since 1962 and takes about **3 to 5 minutes** to reach the top station, a short walk below the tower. It is the most scenic option and the one most first-time visitors choose.

**Getting to the lower station (83 Sopa-ro, Jung-gu):**

1. Go to **Myeongdong Station, Line 4**, and leave by **Exit 4**.
2. Walk straight up to the Hoehyeon intersection and turn left toward Namsan Tunnel No. 3.
3. At the tunnel entrance, take the free **Namsan Oreumi** inclined elevator. It drops you just below the cable car station.
4. Buy your ticket at the station and join the queue.

From the subway to the station takes about 10 to 15 minutes. If you would rather not climb at all, the cable car company runs a **free shuttle** from near **Myeongdong Station Exit 1**, roughly every 10 minutes between about **12:00 p.m. and 8:30 p.m.**, according to the operator's site.

On weekend evenings and around sunset, the queue for the cable car can be long, sometimes an hour in winter. A popular plan is to ride up and walk down, which costs 12,000 won and avoids the return queue.

### 2. Namsan circulation bus 01A / 01B (cheapest)

The small green **01A and 01B** buses (marked with the Haechi mascot) loop up the mountain and stop at the tower plaza. You pay the normal Seoul city bus fare with a T-money card or another transit card. **The buses do not take cash**, so load your card first; our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/) explains how.

| Bus | How often | Main stops |
|---|---|---|
| 01A | Every 6 to 9 minutes | Namsan Yejang parking lot, Chungmuro Station Exit 2, Dongguk Univ. Station, tower |
| 01B | Every 15 to 18 minutes | Namsan Yejang, Chungmuro Station Exit 2, Dongguk Univ. Station, National Theater, tower, Namsan Library, cable car |

The easiest stops to board from are **Chungmuro Station (Lines 3 and 4), Exit 2**, in front of Daehan Cinema, and **Dongguk University Station (Line 3), Exit 6**. From there the ride to the top is about 10 to 15 minutes. Private cars and taxis are not allowed up the final road, so the bus is the only vehicle that reaches the plaza.

### 3. Walk up

Namsan is a gentle hill, and walking up takes 30 to 40 minutes on paved paths and stairs. The official site lists several start points:

- **From Namsan Public Library** (south-west side): about 30 minutes.
- **From the National Theater** (east side, near Dongguk University Station): about 30 to 40 minutes on the southern circuit road.
- **From the stairs near the cable car station** (Myeongdong side): steep steps, roughly 20 to 30 minutes.

The paths are lit at night and busy with locals out for exercise. In winter, steps can be icy after snow, so wear shoes with grip; see our [winter clothing guide](../korea-winter-weather-what-to-wear/) for what to wear on a cold evening.

### 4. Taxi to the base

A taxi can take you to the lower cable car station, the National Theater or Namsan Library, but not to the top. Our [Kakao T taxi guide](../taxi-in-korea-kakao-t-guide/) covers how to call one.

## At the top: what to see

- **Locks of Love:** the fences and lock "trees" on the terrace below the tower are covered in padlocks left by couples. There is no fee. Locks and pens are sold from machines and shops nearby, or bring your own.
- **Palgakjeong pavilion and the beacon towers:** the old signal fire towers (bongsudae) and the pavilion are just beside the plaza and make a good photo stop.
- **The observatory:** the indoor deck has floor-to-ceiling windows, with distance markers to world cities painted on the glass. Allow 30 to 60 minutes.
- **Restaurants and cafés:** the tower has cafés and sit-down restaurants. They are not cheap, so many people eat in Myeongdong before or after.
- **Tower lights:** after dark the tower is lit in colours. On days with good air quality it often glows blue, and other colours are used for events and campaigns.

## Best time to go

- **Sunset into night** is the most popular time. In late November and December, sunset in Seoul is around 5:15 p.m., so arriving by about 4:30 p.m. lets you see the city in daylight, at sunset and lit up.
- **Weekday afternoons** are the quietest.
- **Spring and autumn** bring cherry blossoms and autumn leaves along the Namsan paths.
- **Avoid** the evening queues on Saturdays and around holidays, such as Christmas and New Year's Eve, unless you plan to walk or take the bus. If you are in town for the holidays, see our guides to [Christmas in Seoul](../christmas-in-seoul-markets-lights/) and [New Year's Eve at Bosingak](../new-years-eve-seoul-bosingak-bell/).

The top of Namsan is about 240 metres above the city and much windier than the streets below. In winter it can feel several degrees colder, so bring a hat and gloves.

A note for future visits: the City of Seoul has planned a public gondola from Myeongdong to Namsan, but as of November 2026 it is not open, and the cable car remains the only aerial route.

## FAQ

**Do I need a ticket to visit N Seoul Tower?**
No. The plaza, love locks and views around the base are free. You only need a ticket for the indoor observatory and for the cable car.

**How much is N Seoul Tower plus the cable car?**
Bought separately at the counters, an adult observatory ticket (29,000 won) and a round-trip cable car ticket (15,000 won) come to 44,000 won, as of November 2026. Combo tickets on travel apps are usually cheaper.

**Can I pay by card?**
Yes, the observatory and cable car accept cards. The 01A/01B buses only take transit cards. See our [guide to paying in Korea](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/).

**How long should I plan?**
About 2 to 3 hours from Myeongdong, including the trip up, the plaza and the observatory. Add an hour if you walk both ways.

**Is it open on public holidays?**
Yes. The observatory and cable car run every day of the year, including Lunar New Year and Chuseok, though hours can change for weather or events.

**Is N Seoul Tower good for kids and wheelchair users?**
Yes. The bus reaches the plaza without stairs, and the tower has lifts to the observatory. Children under 36 months ride the cable car and enter the observatory for free.
