Before you fly to South Korea without a visa, you need one of two free-or-cheap online forms: a **K-ETA** (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization) or an **e-Arrival Card**. Which one depends on your passport, your age and when you travel. The rules changed on 1 January 2026, and they may change again on 1 January 2027, so this is worth checking a few weeks before you book.

> **Quick answer:** If you hold a passport from one of the **22 temporarily exempt countries** (including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan and most of Western Europe) and you arrive **by 31 December 2026**, you do **not** need a K-ETA. You do need to fill in the free **e-Arrival Card** at e-arrivalcard.go.kr within **three days before you land**. Everyone else who enters visa-free needs a **K-ETA** (₩10,000, valid three years) from k-eta.go.kr, applied for at least **72 hours before boarding**. A valid K-ETA replaces the arrival card. For trips from **1 January 2027**, no extension of the exemption had been announced as of October 2026.

Rules below are as of October 2026. Always confirm on the official sites before you travel, because the exemption has so far been decided only a week or two before it expired.

## K-ETA vs e-Arrival Card at a glance

| | K-ETA | e-Arrival Card |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Pre-travel authorization for visa-free visitors | Digital version of the old paper arrival card |
| Official site | k-eta.go.kr or the official K-ETA app | e-arrivalcard.go.kr |
| Cost | ₩10,000 (paid by card, not refunded) | Free |
| When to apply | At least 72 hours before boarding | Within 3 days before arrival (counting the arrival day) |
| Valid for | 3 years, or until your passport expires if sooner; multiple trips | One arrival |
| Who needs it | Visa-free nationals who are not exempt | Foreign visitors who do not hold a valid K-ETA |
| Checked by | Your airline at check-in, and immigration | Immigration on arrival |

The two forms are alternatives, not a pair: if you have a valid K-ETA, you skip the e-Arrival Card. If you are exempt from K-ETA and do not apply for one, you fill in the e-Arrival Card instead.

## Who is exempt from K-ETA until 31 December 2026

Korea's Ministry of Justice extended a temporary K-ETA exemption for nationals of **22 countries and regions** from 1 January to 31 December 2026 (Korean time). The list has not changed since 2023:

| Region | Exempt countries and regions |
|---|---|
| Asia | Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao, Singapore |
| Americas | United States (including Guam), Canada |
| Europe | United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Poland, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Denmark |
| Oceania | Australia, New Zealand |

When you start a K-ETA application and choose one of these nationalities, the official site shows a "K-ETA exempt" pop-up. That is your confirmation.

**Some travelers never need a K-ETA, whatever their nationality.** These include visitors aged **17 or under** or **65 or over** on the day they enter, holders of a valid Korean visa or residence card, diplomatic and official passport holders, APEC Business Travel Card holders, aircraft and ship crew on duty, and transit passengers who stay airside.

**"Visa-free" is not the same as "K-ETA exempt."** More than 100 nationalities can visit Korea without a visa, but only the 22 above are on the temporary exemption. If your country is visa-free but not on the list (for example Ireland, Switzerland, Thailand, Malaysia or Mexico), you need a K-ETA before you board. Nationals of countries that need a visa, such as China, Vietnam, the Philippines, Indonesia or India, apply for a visa at a Korean embassy instead; K-ETA does not apply to them.

## What happens for trips from 1 January 2027?

As of October 2026, Korea had **not** announced whether the exemption will be extended into 2027. The previous extensions were each announced in late December, shortly before the old period ran out.

What that means in practice:

- **Arriving on or before 31 December 2026:** The current exemption covers you if you are on the list.
- **Arriving on or after 1 January 2027:** If no extension is announced, nationals of the 22 countries would need a K-ETA like everyone else. Your arrival date is what counts, not the date you booked.
- **Not sure and flying early in 2027?** You can apply for a K-ETA now. It costs ₩10,000, lasts three years and also saves you the e-Arrival Card on every trip. The downside: if the exemption is extended, the fee is not refunded.

For a trip over the New Year, check k-eta.go.kr in the last week of December, or simply apply for a K-ETA at least a few days before you fly.

## How to apply for a K-ETA

1. **Go to the official site** at **k-eta.go.kr** or download the official **K-ETA** app. Type the address yourself rather than clicking ads; unofficial sites charge many times the real fee.
2. **Have ready:** your passport, a recent photo of your face (the app can take one), an email address, your travel dates and your accommodation address in Korea.
3. **Fill in the form** in English. Answer every question honestly; an inconsistent answer is a common reason for delays.
4. **Pay ₩10,000** by credit card. A small card processing charge may be added. The fee is not refunded if you are refused.
5. **Wait for the result** by email or on the site. Most applications are decided within 72 hours, but this is not guaranteed, so apply **at least 72 hours before boarding**, earlier around holidays.

There is no printout and no sticker. The approval is linked to your passport number, and the airline checks it electronically at check-in. If you renew your passport, you need a new K-ETA.

**If you are refused:** You cannot use visa-free entry for that trip. You would need to apply for a visa at a Korean embassy or consulate. A K-ETA also does not guarantee entry; the immigration officer at the airport still makes the final decision.

## How to fill in the e-Arrival Card

Since **1 January 2026**, the paper arrival card handed out on planes is gone. Foreign visitors without a K-ETA complete the e-Arrival Card online instead.

1. **Open e-arrivalcard.go.kr** on your phone or computer. It is free; there is nothing to pay.
2. **Submit it within three days before arrival.** The three days count your arrival day, in Korean time. If you land on a Friday, you can submit from Wednesday.
3. **Enter:** your passport details, flight number, the address where you will stay in Korea and your contact details.
4. **Save the confirmation.** Keep a screenshot in case immigration asks for it.

If you forget, it is not the end of the trip: you can fill in the form at kiosks or on your phone after landing. At busy times at Incheon, that can add a long wait before you even reach the immigration queue, so it is better done the evening before you fly.

## Which do I need? Common cases

| You are | Arriving | You need |
|---|---|---|
| US, UK, Canadian, Australian or Japanese adult | By 31 Dec 2026 | e-Arrival Card (or a K-ETA if you prefer) |
| US, UK, Canadian, Australian or Japanese adult | From 1 Jan 2027 | Check k-eta.go.kr; a K-ETA unless the exemption is extended |
| Irish, Swiss, Thai or Malaysian adult (visa-free, not exempt) | Any date | K-ETA, at least 72 hours before boarding |
| Any visa-free national aged 17 or under or 65 or over | Any date | No K-ETA; e-Arrival Card |
| Holder of a Korean visa | Any date | No K-ETA; e-Arrival Card unless your visa type is exempted |
| Transiting at Incheon without passing immigration | Any date | Neither |

## Staying length and other entry basics

K-ETA and the e-Arrival Card are permissions to board and to arrive. They do not change how long you can stay. Most exempt nationalities, including Americans, Britons and Japanese, can stay **up to 90 days** visa-free for tourism, while Canadians can stay up to six months; a few nationalities have 30-day limits. You still need a passport valid for your stay, and immigration may ask for an onward ticket or proof of where you are staying.

## FAQ

**Do Americans need a K-ETA for Korea in 2026?**
No, not if you arrive by 31 December 2026. The US is on the temporary exemption list. You do need to submit the free e-Arrival Card within three days before you land, unless you choose to get a K-ETA.

**Do I need a K-ETA if I fly in January 2027?**
As of October 2026, no extension had been announced, so plan for needing one unless that changes. Check k-eta.go.kr in late December, or apply in advance for ₩10,000 to be safe.

**How much does a K-ETA cost?**
₩10,000, roughly US$7–8 depending on the exchange rate, as of October 2026. Sites asking for $30, $50 or more are unofficial agents, not the Korean government.

**How long is a K-ETA valid?**
Three years from approval, or until your passport expires if that is sooner. You can use it for any number of trips in that time.

**Is the e-Arrival Card free?**
Yes. The official site is e-arrivalcard.go.kr. You should never be asked to pay for it.

**Do children need a K-ETA?**
No. Travelers aged 17 or under on the day of entry are exempt from K-ETA. They fill in the e-Arrival Card like other visitors without a K-ETA.

**Can I apply for a K-ETA after I land?**
No. It is checked before you board your flight to Korea, so it must be approved before departure.

**If I have a K-ETA, do I still fill in the e-Arrival Card?**
No. A valid K-ETA replaces the arrival card for every trip during its validity.
