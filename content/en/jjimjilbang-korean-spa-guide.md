A jjimjilbang (찜질방) is a Korean bathhouse and sauna complex, usually open 24 hours, where you bathe in gender-separated pools and then relax in shared heated rooms wearing a provided T-shirt and shorts. It is one of the cheapest and most local things a visitor can do in Korea, and it is especially good in winter.

> **Quick answer:** Pay at the front desk (roughly **₩10,000–₩18,000** for adults in Seoul as of November 2026, a little more at night or on weekends), take your locker key and uniform, undress fully in the single-sex locker room, **shower before entering any bath**, soak, then put on the uniform and head to the co-ed sauna floors. Swimsuits are not worn in the baths. Most places let you stay overnight on the floor for the entry fee, usually with a base time of around 12 hours. Extras such as a body scrub, food and massage are paid when you leave.

## What is a jjimjilbang?

There are two related places, and the names matter when you search on a map app:

| Type | Korean | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Bathhouse | 목욕탕 (mogyoktang) / 사우나 (sauna) | Single-sex baths, showers and a dry sauna only. Cheaper, quicker. |
| Jjimjilbang | 찜질방 | The baths, plus co-ed floors with heated rooms, a sleeping area, snack bar, often a gym, PC corner or TV lounge. |

The heated rooms are the main event. Typical ones are a **bulgama (불가마)**, a hot kiln-style room that can be very hot, a **salt or jade room** at a gentler temperature, a **charcoal room**, and an **ice room** for cooling down. Koreans go with friends, couples and families to sweat, eat snacks and nap, so the co-ed floors feel more like a living room than a spa.

## How much does a jjimjilbang cost?

Each venue sets its own prices, so check the board at the counter. As a guide, as of November 2026:

| Item | Typical price in Seoul |
|---|---|
| Jjimjilbang entry, daytime | about ₩10,000–₩14,000 |
| Jjimjilbang entry, night or weekend | about ₩13,000–₩18,000 |
| Bathhouse only (no sauna floors) | cheaper than a jjimjilbang, often under ₩10,000 |
| Uniform and towels | usually included; some places charge a few thousand won |
| Body scrub (seshin, 세신) | about ₩20,000–₩35,000 at a traditional bathhouse |
| Extra time beyond the base period | often ₩1,000 per hour |

Night entry costs more because it includes sleeping there. Children are cheaper, and very young children are often free. Some venues sell discounted tickets through booking apps, but on-site prices are normally only a little higher. Cards are accepted at most front desks; see our [guide to paying in Korea](../paying-in-korea-cards-cash-wowpass/).

## Step by step: your first visit

1. **Take off your shoes at the entrance.** Put them in a small shoe locker and keep its key.
2. **Pay at the front desk.** Hand over the shoe key if asked. You receive a **locker key on a wristband**, a uniform (T-shirt and shorts, colour often differs for men and women) and one or two small towels. The wristband key is also used to charge food and services to your tab.
3. **Go to the locker room for your sex.** Store your clothes and bag. Staff and other guests will be naked here; this is normal.
4. **Shower first.** Wash your whole body, and your hair if it is long, at the seated or standing showers before going into any pool. Soap and shampoo are usually provided, though cheaper places may have only body wash.
5. **Soak.** Expect a hot pool, a warm pool and a cold plunge pool, plus a dry sauna and a steam room. Keep your small towel out of the water; people often fold it on their head. Tie up long hair.
6. **Dry off and change.** Put on the uniform in the locker room.
7. **Go up to the co-ed floors.** Try the heated rooms, rest on the floor (wooden blocks or mats are used as pillows), and drink plenty of water.
8. **Leave when you like.** Return the uniform and towels, then settle extras on your locker tab at the front desk and collect your shoes.

## What to bring

Not much. Most places provide towels, soap and a uniform. Useful extras:

- **Toiletries you are particular about**, such as conditioner, face wash or a hair tie
- **Skin care**, since only basic lotion may be provided
- **A small amount of cash or a card** for snacks and services
- **Earplugs and an eye mask** if you plan to sleep there
- **A swimsuit** only if the venue has an outdoor or mixed pool; it is never worn in the single-sex baths

## Rules and etiquette

- **Nudity is required in the baths.** Swimwear and underwear are not worn in the single-sex bathing areas. Nobody stares, and many visitors find it stops feeling strange within minutes.
- **No photos** anywhere near the locker rooms or baths.
- **Keep your voice down,** especially in sleeping areas at night.
- **Do not wash clothes, dye hair or shave** in the pools. Shave at the showers if you need to.
- **Children:** under Korean public bath rules, children from about age 4 must use the bath of their own sex. Venues often use a height guide such as 90 cm.
- **Teenagers at night:** under-19s may not stay in a jjimjilbang between 22:00 and 05:00 without a parent or guardian.
- **Outside food** is usually not allowed; drinks may be.
- **Lost keys** are charged, at some large spas ₩30,000.

### Can you go to a jjimjilbang with tattoos?

Usually, yes. Korea has no national ban on tattooed guests at bathhouses, and most jjimjilbang in Seoul do not turn away visitors with small or moderate tattoos. Some venues post a rule against tattoos that "cause discomfort to others", which in practice is aimed at very large pieces. If you have extensive tattoos, check with the venue first or cover them with the uniform on the co-ed floors.

## Things to try inside

- **Baked eggs (맥반석 계란, maekbanseok gyeran):** brown, smoky eggs baked in the heated rooms. A small set costs a few thousand won.
- **Sikhye (식혜):** a sweet, cold rice drink and the classic pairing with the eggs.
- **The "sheep's head" towel (양머리, yangmeori):** rolling a towel into a lamb-ear headband, made famous by Korean TV dramas.
- **A body scrub (세신, seshin):** staff scrub you with an exfoliating mitt in the bath area until layers of dead skin come off. Book at the counter in the bath area; it is done in your own sex's section.
- **Korean food:** most jjimjilbang have a snack bar serving ramyeon, cold noodles or rice dishes.

## Sleeping at a jjimjilbang overnight

Many jjimjilbang are open 24 hours and let you sleep in a shared room for the cost of a night ticket. This is a common budget option after a late night out or before an early train. Expect thin mats on a heated floor, lights that never go fully off and some snoring. Women-only sleeping rooms are common. Keep valuables in your locker. It is fine for a night, but book a proper room if you need real sleep; see [where to stay in Seoul](../where-to-stay-in-seoul/).

## FAQ

**Do I have to be naked?**
In the single-sex baths and showers, yes. On the co-ed floors everyone wears the provided uniform.

**Can couples or mixed groups go together?**
Yes. You split up for the baths and meet again on the co-ed floors in uniform.

**How long should I stay?**
Two to three hours is enough for a first visit: about an hour in the baths and the rest on the sauna floors.

**Is a jjimjilbang safe for solo female travelers?**
They are widely used by Korean women, often alone, and many have women-only sleeping rooms. Use your locker for valuables, as you would anywhere.

**Can I go with a cold or right after drinking?**
Skip the hottest rooms after drinking alcohol, and stay out of the pools if you are unwell. Drink water between sauna rooms.

**Is it busy on holidays?**
Yes. Weekends and long holidays such as Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok are the busiest times, and some venues charge holiday prices.
