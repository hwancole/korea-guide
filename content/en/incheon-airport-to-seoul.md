Incheon International Airport (ICN) sits on an island about 50 km west of central Seoul, so the trip into the city takes roughly 45 minutes to 1.5 hours depending on how you go. There are four realistic options, and the best one depends mostly on where your hotel is and what time you land.

> **Quick answer:** For most visitors, the **AREX All-Stop train** is the best value: about ₩5,000 with a T-money card and roughly an hour to Seoul Station, with stops at Gimpo Airport, Hongdae and Gongdeok. If your hotel is near Seoul Station and you want a guaranteed seat, take the **AREX Express** (₩13,000, 43 minutes from Terminal 1). If you have heavy luggage and your hotel is far from a subway line, an **airport limousine bus** (about ₩17,000–18,000) often drops you closer to the door.

Fares below are as of October 2026. Korean transport fares change every year or two, so treat them as a guide and check the screen at the machine or counter.

## Option 1: AREX All-Stop train (cheapest)

AREX (Airport Railroad Express) runs two kinds of trains on the same line. The All-Stop train is a regular commuter train that stops at every station between the airport and Seoul Station, including **Gimpo Airport**, **Digital Media City**, **Hongik University (Hongdae)** and **Gongdeok**, all of which connect to Seoul subway lines.

- **Fare:** ₩5,350 for an adult from Terminal 2 to Seoul Station with a T-money or other transit card, according to AREX's official fare table. From Terminal 1 it is slightly cheaper. A single-journey ticket adds ₩100 plus a ₩500 deposit that you get back at the end.
- **Time:** About 59 minutes from Terminal 1 and 66 minutes from Terminal 2.
- **Hours:** The first trains leave a little after 5 a.m. and the last ones around midnight. Check the exact last departure on the AREX timetable if you land late.
- **How to ride:** Follow the "Trains" or "Airport Railroad" signs to the Transportation Center (B1 floor at both terminals). Buy a T-money card at a convenience store first, top it up with cash, then tap in at the gates.

Because it is part of the integrated metro fare system, you can transfer to the Seoul subway with a T-money card and pay only a small distance-based extra. For how T-money works, see our [Seoul subway guide](../seoul-subway-guide/).

**Downsides:** It is a commuter train with side-facing bench seats and limited luggage space. At rush hour (roughly 7–9 a.m. and 6–8 p.m.) the Seoul end can get crowded.

## Option 2: AREX Express train (fastest to Seoul Station)

The Express train runs non-stop between the two airport terminals and Seoul Station, with reserved forward-facing seats and luggage racks.

| | Express train |
|---|---|
| Adult fare | ₩13,000 (official discounted fare; the list fare is higher) |
| Child fare (6–12) | ₩9,500 |
| Terminal 1 to Seoul Station | 43 minutes |
| Terminal 2 to Seoul Station | 51 minutes |
| Frequency | Roughly every 30–40 minutes |

**Where to buy:** At the AREX Express ticket counters and machines in the Transportation Center at either terminal, on the AREX website or app, or through travel booking platforms, which sometimes sell vouchers a little below the counter price.

**Is it worth it?** Only if Seoul Station is actually where you need to be (or one quick transfer from it). If your hotel is in Hongdae or Mapo, the All-Stop train is cheaper and gets you there without going all the way into Seoul Station. Note also that the Seoul **Climate Card** tourist pass does not cover the Express train.

## Option 3: Airport limousine bus

Airport limousine buses run from both terminals to dozens of stops across Seoul, including Myeongdong, Jongno, Gangnam, Jamsil and Dongdaemun, and many stop directly outside major hotels.

- **Fare:** About ₩17,000 to ₩18,000 one-way for an adult to central Seoul, depending on the operator and route (as of October 2026). Children pay less.
- **Time:** 60–100 minutes, very dependent on traffic. Friday evenings and holiday weekends are slowest.
- **Tickets:** Sold at the limousine bus ticket counters and kiosks in the arrivals area. Ask the counter which route and stop number serves your hotel.
- **Where to board:** Bus stops are just outside the arrivals hall on 1F at Terminal 1 and B1 at Terminal 2. Each route has a numbered stop; the ticket counter staff will tell you which.

**Why choose it:** The driver loads your suitcase underneath, you sit in a reclining seat the whole way, and you skip subway transfers and stairs. If your hotel is in Gangnam or Jamsil, the bus is often more convenient than the train.

## Option 4: Taxi

Taxis wait outside the arrivals hall at both terminals. A regular metered taxi to central Seoul usually costs in the range of ₩65,000 to ₩90,000 including highway tolls, depending on your destination, traffic and the time of day. Deluxe (black) taxis and larger "jumbo" vans cost more. A late-night surcharge applies.

For visitors, the **International Taxi** service is worth knowing about: drivers speak English, Japanese or Chinese, and rides from the airport to Seoul are charged a flat fare by zone. You can book through its call center (1644-2255) or ask at the International Taxi desk in the arrivals hall at either terminal.

**When a taxi makes sense:** When there are three or four of you splitting the fare, when you land after the last train, or when you are traveling with small children and lots of bags. Most taxis accept foreign credit cards, but check before you set off.

## Landing late at night

If your flight arrives after about 11 p.m., the last trains may be gone by the time you clear immigration. Your options:

1. **Late-night airport buses.** A few night routes (N6000-series) run from Incheon into Seoul after the regular buses stop. Check the airport's "Seoul Late-Night Buses" page for the current schedule.
2. **Taxi.** Always available, but expect the late-night surcharge.
3. **Stay at the airport.** Both terminals have 24-hour seating areas, and there are transit hotels and capsule hotels inside the airport.

## Which option should I choose?

- **Hotel in Hongdae, Mapo or near Gongdeok:** AREX All-Stop train.
- **Hotel near Seoul Station or Namdaemun:** AREX Express if you want comfort, All-Stop if you want to save ₩8,000.
- **Hotel in Myeongdong:** Limousine bus (direct stop) or All-Stop train to Seoul Station then subway Line 4.
- **Hotel in Gangnam, Jamsil or COEX:** Limousine bus.
- **Group of three or four with bags, or arriving after midnight:** Taxi.

## FAQ

**Can I use a credit card to pay for the AREX All-Stop train?**
Not by tapping a foreign card at the gate. Use a T-money card (loaded with cash) or buy a single-journey ticket at the machine. Express tickets can be bought by card at the counter or online.

**Does the Climate Card cover Incheon Airport?**
No. The Seoul Climate Card is designed for travel inside Seoul and does not cover the AREX Express. Check the Seoul city site for exactly which AREX stretches it includes before relying on it for the airport.

**Is Terminal 1 or Terminal 2 my terminal?**
It depends on your airline, and several airlines have moved between terminals in recent years, so check your ticket or the airport website rather than an old guide. The two terminals are connected by a free shuttle bus and by AREX trains.

**How long does immigration take at Incheon?**
It varies with the time of day and how many flights land together. Allow extra time if you need to catch the last train, and have your K-ETA or arrival details ready if they apply to you.

**Can I buy a SIM card or T-money card at the airport?**
Yes. Convenience stores in the arrivals halls sell T-money cards, and telecom counters sell SIM cards and eSIM pickups.
